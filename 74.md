## Introduction (paras 46.1-46.3)

<a id="s295-chap_46_navPoint_2"></a>
### Objective of hedge accounting

<a id="s295-Para_46.1"></a>**46.1** The objective of hedge accounting is to represent the effect of risk management activities that use financial instruments to manage exposures arising from particular risks that could affect profit or loss (P&amp;L) or other comprehensive income (OCI).

[IFRS 9 para 6.1.1].

[FAQ 46.1.1 – What is hedge accounting?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.1.1)

<a id="s295-chap_46_navPoint_3"></a>
### Interaction with macro hedging

 

<a id="s295-Para_46.2"></a>**46.2**An entity could choose not to apply IFRS 9 to fair value hedges of the interest rate exposure of a portfolio of financial assets or liabilities (commonly referred to as ‘fair value macro hedges’). The IASB has a separate project to address the accounting for macro hedges. Until this project is completed, entities using IFRS 9 for hedge accounting can either apply IFRS 9 or continue to apply IAS 39 requirements for fair value macro hedges (see chapter 6.8A). This is only applicable for fair value hedges of the interest rate exposure of a portfolio of financial assets or liabilities, and cannot be applied, by analogy, to other types of hedges. IFRS 9 addresses the accounting for other hedges of groups of items, including those that constitute a gross or net position (see [para 46.58](74.md#s298-Para_46.58) onwards for further details).

[IFRS 9 para 6.1.3].

[FAQ 46.2.1 – Why is macro hedging addressed as a separate project?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.2.1)

 

<a id="s295-chap_46_navPoint_4"></a>
### Accounting policy choice

<a id="s295-Para_46.3"></a>**46.3** IFRS 9 provides an accounting policy choice: entities can either continue to apply the hedge accounting requirements of IAS 39 (see chapter 6.8A), pending the macro hedging project being finalised, or they can apply IFRS 9. If IFRS 9 is applied, it can be applied either in its entirety, or with the exception of the guidance for fair value macro hedges of interest rate risk, as described in [paragraph 46.2](74.md#s295-Para_46.2). This accounting policy choice will apply to all hedge accounting and cannot be made on a hedge-by-hedge basis. An entity that has chosen to continue to apply IAS 39 can change its policy to apply IFRS 9 at the beginning of any reporting period. This accounting policy choice applies to the application of hedge accounting only, and has no impact on the implementation of the other two phases of IFRS 9 namely, ‘classification and measurement’ and ‘impairment’.

[IFRS 9 para 7.2.21].

## Accounting for hedges (paras 46.4 - 46.32)

 

<a id="s296-Para_46.4"></a>**46.4** IFRS 9 allows three hedge accounting models:

- fair value hedge;

- cash flow hedge; and

- net investment hedge.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.2].

<a id="s296-Para_46.4.1"></a>**46.4.1** In September 2019, the IASB issued amendments to IFRS 9 (and IAS 39) to provide temporary relief from applying specific hedge accounting requirements to hedging relationships directly affected by IBOR reform followed by further amendments ‘IBOR reform and its effects on financial reporting – Phase 2’ in August 2020. These reliefs should permit hedge accounting to continue in many circumstances. For further details, see [Appendix 3](88.md#s370-chap_Appendix_3), as well as our In depth publications, ‘Practical guide to Phase 1 amendments IFRS 9, IAS 39 and IFRS 7 for IBOR reform’ and ‘Practical guide to Phase 2 amendments to IFRS 9, IAS 39, IFRS 7, IFRS 4 and IFRS 16 for IBOR reform.

<a id="s296-chap_46_navPoint_6"></a>
### Fair value hedge

 

<a id="s296-Para_46.5"></a>**46.5** The risk being hedged in a fair value hedge is a change in the fair value of an asset or liability or an unrecognised firm commitment that is attributable to a particular risk and could affect P&amp;L (or, for equity investments accounted for at fair value through other comprehensive income (FVOCI), could affect OCI). Changes in fair value might arise through changes in interest rates (for fixed-rate loans), foreign exchange rates, equity prices or commodity prices. A fair value hedge is accounted for as follows:

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.2(a)]. [IFRS 9 para 6.5.3].

[EX 46.5.1 – Examples of fair value hedges](75.md#s303-EX46.5.1)  
[FAQ 46.5.2 – Can an entity designate a basis swap in a fair value hedge of a floating rate financial instrument?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.5.2)  
[FAQ 46.5.3 – Is it permissible to designate a fair value hedging relationship with an inflation linked basis swap?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.5.3)  
[EX 46.5.4 - Hedging insurance contract liabilities](75.md#s303-EX46.5.4)

**a) Adjustments to hedged items**

<a id="s296-Para_46.6"></a>**46.6** The carrying value of the hedged item is adjusted for fair value changes attributable to the risk being hedged. Those fair value changes are recognised in P&amp;L, except for those attributable to equity instruments accounted for at FVOCI, in which case they are recognised in OCI.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.8(b)].

<a id="s296-Para_46.7"></a>**46.7** This adjustment is often referred to as a ‘basis adjustment in a fair value hedge’, because the hedging gain or loss adjusts the carrying value of the hedged item, resulting in an amount that is neither cost nor fair value.

**1) Basis adjustment – non-financial items**

<a id="s296-Para_46.8"></a>**46.8** In a fair value hedge where the hedged item is a non-financial item, the changes in fair value attributable to the risk being hedged are adjusted against the carrying value of such non-financial item. For example, in a fair value hedge of inventory, the changes in fair value attributable to the risk being hedged are adjusted against the carrying value of the inventory, and the adjusted carrying amount becomes the cost basis for the purposes of applying the ‘lower of cost and net realisable value’ test under IAS 2. In other words, the basis adjustment remains part of the carrying value of inventory and enters into the determination of earnings when the inventory is sold.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.8(b)].

**2) Basis adjustment – debt instruments measured at amortised cost**

<a id="s296-Para_46.9"></a>**46.9** Where the hedged item is an interest-bearing financial instrument for which the effective interest rate method of accounting is used, any adjustment to the carrying amount of the hedged financial instrument should be amortised to P&amp;L. The adjustment should be based on a recalculated effective interest rate at the date the amortisation begins and should be fully amortised by maturity. Amortisation can begin as soon as an adjustment exists and should begin no later than when the hedged item ceases to be adjusted for changes in its fair value attributable to the risk being hedged. The IASB decided to permit entities to defer amortisation of a basis adjustment until the hedged interest-bearing financial instrument ceases to be basis adjusted, in order to simplify the accounting and record keeping that an entity might otherwise have to undertake to track and properly account for adjustments that would otherwise reverse through the ongoing application of fair value hedge accounting.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.10].

[FAQ 46.9.1 – What is the impact of deferring amortisation of the basis adjustment?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.9.1)

[FAQ 46.9.2 - What is the pull-to-par adjustment and when would it be required?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.9.2)

**3) Basis adjustment – debt instruments measured at FVOCI**

<a id="s296-Para_46.10"></a>**46.10** Amortisation applies in the same manner as to debt instruments measured at amortised cost, but the basis adjustment is made to the amount that represents the cumulative gain or loss previously recognised, instead of by adjusting the carrying amount.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.10].

**4) Basis adjustment – firm commitments**

<a id="s296-Para_46.11"></a>**46.11** Where the hedged item is an unrecognised firm commitment, the cumulative change in fair value is recognised as an asset or liability, with a corresponding gain or loss in P&amp;L. When the firm commitment is fulfilled, the initial carrying amount of the asset or liability is adjusted to include the cumulative change in the firm commitment that was previously recognised in the balance sheet.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.8(b)]. [IFRS 9 para 6.5.9].

**b) Accounting for hedging instruments**

<a id="s296-Para_46.12"></a>**46.12**The hedging instrument is generally measured at fair value, with changes in fair value also recognised in P&amp;L. The only exception to this requirement is for fair value hedges of an equity instrument accounted for at FVOCI. The gains and losses of equity instruments are never recycled to P&amp;L, so changes in the fair value of the hedging instrument are also recorded in OCI without recycling to P&amp;L.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.8(a)].

[FAQ 46.12.1 – Can the amounts recorded in OCI as a result of changes in fair value of the hedging instrument in a fair value hedge of an equity instrument designated at FVOCI be transferred to other components of equity?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.12.1)

<a id="s296-Para_46.13"></a>**46.13** If the fair value hedge were fully effective, the gain or loss on the hedging instrument would exactly offset the loss or gain on the hedged item attributable to the risk being hedged. There is no net effect in P&amp;L. However, this is rarely the case, and often some difference arises. The recognition of this difference in P&amp;L is commonly referred to as ‘hedge ineffectiveness’ ([see para 46.37](74.md#s297-Para_46.37) onwards for further details).

 

<a id="s296-chap_46_navPoint_7"></a>
### Cash flow hedge

 

<a id="s296-Para_46.14"></a>**46.14** The risk being hedged in a cash flow hedge is the exposure to variability in cash flows that is attributable to a particular risk associated with a recognised asset or liability, an unrecognised firm commitment (currency risk only) or a highly probable forecast transaction, and could affect P&amp;L. Future cash flows might relate to existing assets and liabilities, such as future interest payments or receipts on floating-rate debt. Future cash flows can also relate to highly probable forecast sales or purchases in a foreign currency. Volatility in future cash flows might result from changes in interest rates, exchange rates, equity prices or commodity prices.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.2(b)]. [IFRS 9 para 6.5.4].

[EX 46.14.1– Examples of cash flow hedges](75.md#s303-EX46.14.1)

<a id="s296-Para_46.15"></a>**46.15** Provided the hedge is effective, changes in the fair value of the hedging instrument are initially recognised in OCI and taken to a separate component of equity. Any ineffective portion of the change in the fair value of the hedging instrument is recognised directly in P&amp;L.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.11].

<a id="s296-Para_46.16"></a>**46.16**The amount recognised in the separate component of equity should be the lower of:

- the cumulative gain or loss on the hedging instrument from the inception of the hedge; and

- the cumulative change in the fair value (present value) of the expected cash flows on the hedged item from the inception of the hedge.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.11(a)].

<a id="s296-Para_46.17"></a>**46.17**If the cumulative change in the hedging instrument exceeds the change in the hedged item (sometimes referred to as an ‘over-hedge’), ineffectiveness will be recognised in P&amp;L for the excess. If the cumulative change in the hedging instrument is less than the change in the hedged item (sometimes referred to as an ‘under-hedge’), no ineffectiveness will be recognised. This is different from a fair value hedge, in which ineffectiveness is recognised on both over- and under-hedges.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.11(c)].

<a id="s296-Para_46.18"></a>**46.18** For cash flow hedges of a forecast transaction which subsequently results in the recognition of a non-financial item (such as property, plant and equipment inventory or an IFRS 15 contract liability), or where a hedged forecast transaction for a non-financial asset or a non-financial liability becomes a firm commitment for which fair value hedge accounting is applied, the carrying value of that item must be adjusted for the accumulated gains or losses recognised directly in equity. This is often referred to as a ‘basis adjustment in a cash flow hedge’. This is not a reclassification adjustment, as defined in IAS 1, and hence it does not affect other comprehensive income.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.11(d)(i)].

<a id="s296-Para_46.19"></a>**46.19**For other cash flow hedges, the accumulated gains and losses recorded in equity should be reclassified to P&amp;L as a reclassification adjustment, as defined in IAS 1, in the same period or periods during which the hedged expected future cash flows affect P&amp;L. This might be where interest income or expense is recognised or a forecast sale occurs.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.11(d)(ii)].

<a id="s296-Para_46.20"></a>**46.20**Where there is a cumulative loss on the hedging instrument and it is no longer expected that the loss will be recovered, it must be immediately recognised in P&amp;L.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.11(d)(iii)].

[FAQ 46.20.1 – Does IFRS 9 require debit balances in the cash flow hedge reserve to be tested for recoverability?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.20.1)

<a id="s296-Para_46.21"></a>**46.21** IFRS 9 permits hedge accounting for hedges of the net position of a group of items containing offsetting risk positions, but only in limited situations. See paragraphs [46.59 to 46.63](74.md#s298-Para_46.59) and FAQ 46.100.3 for further details and an example in a group situation.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.6.14]. [IFRS 9 App B para B6.6.15].

 

<a id="s296-chap_46_navPoint_8"></a>
### Net investment hedge

 

<a id="s296-Para_46.22"></a>**46.22**A net investment in a foreign operation is an eligible hedged item. It is defined as “the amount of the reporting entity’s interest in the net assets of that operation”. Such foreign operations might be subsidiaries, associates, joint ventures or branches. An entity might decide to hedge against the effects of changes in exchange rates on its net investment in a foreign operation. This might be done with non-derivative financial liabilities or with derivatives. If hedge accounting is applied, the hedge is accounted for similarly to a cash flow hedge, so the foreign currency gains or losses on the hedging instrument are recognised in OCI and deferred in equity, to the extent that the hedge is effective.

[IFRIC 16 para 1]. [IFRS 9 para 6.5.13]. [IAS 21 para 8].

<a id="s296-Para_46.23"></a>**46.23**Because the net assets of subsidiaries, associates and joint ventures are only reported in the reporting entity’s consolidated financial statements, hedge accounting for a net investment in such foreign operations can only be carried out at the consolidation level. The only exception is where an entity does not have investments in subsidiaries and it prepares financial statements in which investments in associates or joint ventures are accounted for using the equity method or that include a branch or a joint operation (as defined in IFRS 11).

[IFRIC 16 para 2].

<a id="s296-Para_46.24"></a>**46.24**Under IAS 21, the reporting entity’s share of the net assets of a foreign operation is translated into the group’s presentation currency at the closing exchange rate, and all resulting exchange differences are recognised as a separate component in equity until the reporting entity disposes of the foreign operation. The hedge of a net investment in a foreign operation at the consolidated level is, therefore, a hedge of the translation foreign currency risk arising on the foreign operation, which is included in the reporting entity’s financial statements. IFRS 9 and IFRIC 16, ‘Hedges of a net investment in a foreign operation’, provide guidance on hedges of net investments in foreign operations. IFRIC 16 applies only to hedges of net investments in foreign operations; it should not be applied, by analogy, to other types of hedge accounting, such as fair value or cash flow hedge accounting.

[IFRIC 16 para 8].

**a) Hedged items**

<a id="s296-Para_46.25"></a>**46.25**In a hedge of the foreign currency risks arising from a net investment in a foreign operation, the hedged item can be an amount of net assets equal to or less than the carrying amount of the net assets of the foreign operation in the consolidated financial statements of the parent entity. The amount of the net assets that might be designated as the hedged item in the consolidated financial statements of a parent depends on whether any lower-level parent of the foreign operation has applied hedge accounting for all or part of the net assets of that foreign operation and that accounting has been maintained in the parent’s consolidated financial statements. The hedged risk can only be designated as the foreign currency exposure arising between the functional currency of the foreign operation and the functional currency of a parent entity (the immediate, intermediate or ultimate parent entity) of that foreign operation. The fact that the net investment is held through an intermediate parent does not affect the nature of the economic risk arising from the foreign currency exposure to the ultimate parent entity.

[IFRIC 16 para 11]. [IFRIC 16 para 12].

[FAQ 46.25.1 – What amount of a net investment in a foreign operation qualifies as a hedged item in a net investment hedge?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.25.1)  
[FAQ 46.25.2 – Can goodwill be included in a net investment hedge?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.25.2)

<a id="s296-Para_46.26"></a>**46.26**However, the hedged risk must relate to the functional currencies of the entities involved (that is, the foreign operation and any of its parents). IFRIC 16 does not permit an entity to hedge a foreign operation to the group’s presentation currency. This is because IAS 21 places no restrictions on the presentation currency that a group can select, and so presentation currency risk is not viewed as a true economic exposure. In practice, this limitation is likely to have little effect, as most entities either only hedge functional currency risk or choose a presentation currency that is also the functional currency of a relevant parent.

[IFRIC 16 para 10].

<a id="s296-Para_46.27"></a>**46.27**If the same net assets of a foreign operation are hedged by more than one parent entity within the group (for example, both a direct and an indirect parent entity) for the same risk, only one hedging relationship will qualify for hedge accounting in the consolidated financial statements of the ultimate parent. A hedging relationship designated by one parent entity in its consolidated financial statements need not be maintained by another higher-level parent entity. However, if it is not maintained by the higher-level parent entity, the hedge accounting applied by the lower-level parent should be reversed before the higher-level parent’s hedge accounting is recognised.

[IFRIC 16 para 13].

[FAQ 46.27.1 – What factors should be considered for net investment hedges in a group situation? (Question 1 – What options are available for designating a net investment hedge in a group situation?)](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.27.1)

**b) Hedging instruments**

<a id="s296-Para_46.28"></a>**46.28**A derivative or a non-derivative instrument (or a combination of derivative and non-derivative instruments) could be designated as a hedging instrument in a hedge of a net investment in a foreign operation. The hedging instrument(s) can be held by any entity or entities within the group, as long as the designation, documentation and effectiveness requirements of paragraph 6.4.1 of IFRS 9 that relate to a net investment hedge are satisfied. In particular, the hedging strategy of the group should be clearly documented, because of the possibility of different designations at different levels of the group.

[IFRIC 16 para 14].

<a id="s296-Para_46.29"></a>**46.29** The designated risk could be spot foreign exchange risk if the hedging instruments are not derivatives (for example, a debt instrument). If the hedging instruments are forward contracts, an entity can designate either the spot or the forward foreign exchange risk as the hedged risk.

[IFRIC 16 para AG2].

[FAQ 46.29.3 – How is a fixed-fixed cross-currency interest rate swap accounted for in a net investment hedge?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.29.3)

**c) Effectiveness requirements**

<a id="s296-Para_46.30"></a>**46.30** Net investment hedges have the same requirements as cash flow and fair value hedges to demonstrate an economic relationship between the hedged item and the hedging instrument, and to recognise the effective portion of the hedging instrument in OCI and the ineffective portion in P&amp;L. In March 2016, the IFRS IC issued an agenda decision indicating that an entity should apply the ‘lower of’ test used to determine the effective portion of the hedging instrument’s gains or losses for net investment hedges as well as cash flow hedges. This is not expected to have any significant impact on current practice.

[IFRS 9 para 6.4.1(c)]. [IFRS 9 para 6.5.13].

<a id="s296-Para_46.31"></a>**46.31** When determining the effective portion of a hedging instrument in a hedge of a net investment, an entity calculates the gain or loss on the hedging instrument by reference to the functional currency of the parent entity against whose functional currency the hedged risk (that is, the foreign currency risk of the net investment) is measured, in accordance with the hedge documentation. This is the same, regardless of the type of hedging instrument used, the entity in the group holding the hedging instrument and whether that entity applies hedge accounting in its separate financial statements. This ensures that effectiveness is determined on the basis of changes in fair value or cash flows of the hedging instrument, compared with the changes in the net investment, as documented. Any ineffectiveness to be reported is not therefore dependent on the functional currency of the entity holding the instrument. In other words, the fact that some of the change in the hedging instrument is recognised in P&amp;L by one entity within the group, and some is recognised in OCI by another entity, does not affect the assessment of whether the hedge effectiveness requirements continue to be met.

[IFRIC 16 para 15].

**d) Recycling on disposal of foreign operation**

<a id="s296-Para_46.32"></a>**46.32** When a foreign operation that was hedged is disposed of, the amount reclassified or ‘recycled’ to P&amp;L from the foreign currency translation reserve in respect of the hedging instrument is the effective portion of the revaluation of the hedging instrument, calculated in accordance with paragraph 6.5.13(a) of IFRS 9. The amount reclassified to P&amp;L from the foreign currency translation reserve in respect of the net investment in that foreign operation is the amount included in that parent’s foreign currency translation reserve in respect of that foreign operation.

[IAS 21 para 48]. [IFRS 9 para 6.5.14].

## Qualifying criteria for hedge accounting (paras 46.33-46.55)

 

<a id="s297-Para_46.33"></a>**46.33** An entity’s risk management strategy is central to the objective of hedge accounting under IFRS 9. However, hedge accounting is an exception to the normal accounting rules, and so some restrictions are necessary to determine whether or not a proposed hedging relationship qualifies for hedge accounting. An entity is only allowed to apply hedge accounting if it meets the specified qualifying criteria, which are as follows:

- The hedging relationship consists only of eligible hedging instruments ([see para 46.97 onwards](74.md#s299-Para_46.97)) and eligible hedged items ([see para 46.56 onwards](74.md#s298-Para_46.56)).

- There is formal designation and documentation of the hedging relationship and risk management objective and strategy at inception of the hedge ([see para 46.34 onwards](74.md#s297-Para_46.34)).

- The hedge relationship meets the hedge effectiveness requirements ([see para 46.37 onwards](74.md#s297-Para_46.37)).

[IFRS 9 para 6.4.1].

 

<a id="s297-chap_46_navPoint_10"></a>
### Formal designation and documentation

 

For hedges impacted by IBOR reform, entities applying the hedge accounting exceptions as set out in Phase 1 Interest Rate Benchmark Reform Amendments to IFRS 9, IAS 39 and IFRS 7 should refer to [Appendix 3 para A3.41](88.md#s379-Para_A3.41) for the impact of the Phase 2 amendments issued in August 2020.

  

<a id="s297-Para_46.34"></a>**46.34**Formal designation and documentation must be in place at the inception of the hedge relationship. The documentation should identify:

- the risk management objective and strategy;

- the hedged item;

- the hedging instrument;

- the nature of risk being hedged; and

- how management will assess hedge effectiveness, including giving a description of sources of expected ineffectiveness and how the hedge ratio was determined.

[IFRS 9 para 6.4.1(b)].

<a id="s297-Para_46.35"></a>**46.35** Since there must be formal designation and documentation of the hedging relationship at the inception of the hedge, a hedge relationship cannot be designated retrospectively. However, IFRS 9 does not require a hedging relationship to be established at the time the hedging instrument is acquired. For instance, an entity could designate and document a derivative contract as a hedging instrument after entering into the derivative contract, although this might create some ineffectiveness. Hedge accounting will apply prospectively from the date that all hedge accounting criteria in [paragraph 46.33](74.md#s297-Para_46.33) are met, which includes the documentation at inception of the hedge accounting relationship.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.21].

 

<a id="s297-chap_46_navPoint_11"></a>
### Hedge effectiveness

 

For hedges impacted by IBOR reform, where the prospective assessment relief set out in Phase 1 Interest Rate Benchmark Reform Amendments to IFRS 9, IAS 39 and IFRS 7 was applied, refer to [Appendix 3 para A3.25](88.md#s373-Para_A3.25) for further information. Refer to [Appendix 3 para A3.41](88.md#s379-Para_A3.41) for the Phase 2 amendments issued in August 2020.

  

<a id="s297-Para_46.37"></a>**46.37** Hedge effectiveness is defined as the extent to which changes in the fair value or cash flows of the hedging instrument offset changes in the fair value or cash flows of the hedged item.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.4.1].

<a id="s297-Para_46.38"></a>**46.38** IFRS 9 introduces three hedge effectiveness requirements:

- an economic relationship exists between the hedged item and hedging instrument;

- the effect of credit risk does not dominate the value changes that result from the economic relationship; and

- the hedge ratio is the same as that resulting from the quantity of the hedged item that the entity actually hedges and the quantity of the hedging instrument that the entity actually uses to hedge that quantity of hedged item.

[IFRS 9 para 6.4.1.(c)].

<a id="s297-Para_46.39"></a>**46.39** The method of demonstrating hedge effectiveness is not mandated, but it should be documented and, if changed, this fact should be documented, along with the sources of ineffectiveness. Common methods of demonstrating hedge effectiveness include the dollar offset method (see [FAQ 46.39.3 to FAQ 46.39.9](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.39.3)) and regression analysis (see [FAQ 46.39.10](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.39.10)).

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.4.13]. [IFRS 9 App B para B6.4.17]. [IFRS 9 App B para B6.4.19].

<a id="s297-Para_46.40"></a>**46.40** The assessment of whether the hedge meets the effectiveness requirements is forward-looking only. It must be done, at a minimum, at each reporting date and on any significant change in circumstances affecting the hedge. Any ineffectiveness identified must be recorded through P&amp;L.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.4.12].

**a) Economic relationship**

<a id="s297-Para_46.41"></a>**46.41**An economic relationship must exist between the hedged item and the hedging instrument. This means that there must be an expectation that the value of the hedging instrument and the value of the hedged item would generally move in the opposite direction as a result of having the same or economically related underlyings or hedged risk. For example:

- Forecast fixed interest payments on a bond and an interest rate swap that receives fixed interest payments and pays variable interest have the same underlying (the fixed interest rate).

- Forecast Brent crude oil purchases could be hedged with a West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil future, because there is a more liquid market for the latter but the two could be substituted for one another, subject to geographical issues, and management is likely to be able to prove that quantitatively.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.4.4].

<a id="s297-Para_46.41.1"></a>**46.41.1**The IASB also recognised that some entities designate ‘proxy hedges’; and the IFRS IC noted, in its January 2016 agenda decision on transition to IFRS 9, that the use of proxy hedge accounting in cases in which it reflects the entity’s risk management did not appear to be restricted to instances in which IFRS 9 had prohibited an entity from designating hedged items in accordance with its actual risk management.

[IFRS 9 para BC6.96]. [IFRS 9 para BC6.97]. [IFRS 9 para BC6.98]. [IFRS 9 para BC6.99]. [IFRS 9 para BC6.100]. [IFRS 9 para BC6.101]. [January 2016 IFRIC update].

[FAQ 46.41.1.1 – Can an entity hedge account with a different but related underlying (proxy hedge accounting)?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.41.1.1)

<a id="s297-Para_46.42"></a>**46.42** But it is not enough to demonstrate a statistical correlation. The existence of a statistical correlation does not mean that there is an economic relationship. Management should always do a qualitative analysis to demonstrate that there is an economic relationship. If the critical terms of the hedged item and the hedging instrument match, and the changes in fair value and cash flows attributable to the risk being hedged might be expected generally to offset each other fully (both when the hedge is entered into and afterwards), management might not need to go further.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.4.6]. [IFRS 9 App B para B6.4.14].

[FAQ 46.42.1 – Is a quantitative test sufficient to prove an economic relationship?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.42.1)

<a id="s297-Para_46.43"></a>**46.43**The principal terms of the hedging instrument and hedged items are those that are critical to the assessment of hedge effectiveness. For instance, an interest rate swap is likely to be an effective hedge if the notional and principal amounts, term, re-pricing dates, dates of interest and principal receipts and payments, and basis for measuring interest rates are the same for the hedging instrument and the hedged item. In such a case, a qualitative assessment will be sufficient to demonstrate an economic relationship between the swap and the hedged item.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.4.14].

<a id="s297-Para_46.44"></a>**46.44**However, if the critical terms do not match, for example, if the hedging instrument contains additional features such as optionality, a qualitative assessment is unlikely to be sufficient to demonstrate an economic relationship. Hence, management would also need to do a quantitative analysis at each reporting date.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.4.16].

**b) Credit risk**

<a id="s297-Para_46.45"></a>**46.45** Even if there is an economic relationship, a change in the credit risk of the hedging instrument or the hedged item must not be of such magnitude that it dominates the value changes that result from that economic relationship. The effect of credit risk must not dominate the value changes associated with the hedged risk; otherwise, the level of offset might become erratic. However, if the overall fair value movements are very small, this is not deemed to create dominance, even though similarly small credit risk changes might appear significant.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.4.7].

[FAQ 46.45.1 – What factors should be considered when assessing whether credit risk dominates the economic relationship?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.45.1)  
[FAQ 46.45.2 – Does IFRS 9 provide a definition of when credit risk dominates?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.45.2)  
[FAQ 46.45.3 – Is the credit risk assessment applicable for the hedging instrument only?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.45.3)

<a id="s297-Para_46.46"></a>**46.46**Even where credit risk (counterparty and own credit risk) does not dominate the value changes that result from the economic relationship, it might still create ineffectiveness that will need to be included in P&amp;L. An entity must consider the likelihood of default by the counterparty to the hedging instrument in assessing hedge effectiveness. An entity cannot ignore whether it will be able to collect all amounts due under the contractual provisions of the hedging instrument. For a cash flow hedge, the fair value of the underlying cash flow will not be affected by a change in credit risk of the hedge counterparty. However, the fair value of the actual hedging instrument will be affected. For a fair value hedge, if there is a change in the counterparty’s creditworthiness, the fair value of the hedging instrument will change, which affects the assessment of whether the hedge relationship still qualifies for continued hedge accounting. Adjustments to derivative valuations for the entity’s own credit risk, as required by IFRS 13 ([see chapter 5 para 51](11.md#s33-Para_5.51)), will have a similar impact.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.4.8].

**c) Hedge ratio**

<a id="s297-Para_46.47"></a>**46.47** The hedge ratio is the relationship between the quantity of the hedging instrument and the quantity of the hedged item in terms of their relative weighting. The hedge ratio used for hedge accounting purposes should be the same as that used for risk management purposes, so it is primarily a risk management rather than an accounting decision.

[IFRS 9 para 6.4.1(c)]. [IFRS 9 para B6.4.9]. [IFRS 9 App A Defined terms].

<a id="s297-Para_46.48"></a>**46.48** An entity must ensure that the hedge ratio is aligned with that required by the economic hedging strategy (or risk management strategy) and the hedging objective. A deliberate imbalance is not permitted. This requirement is to ensure that entities do not introduce a mismatch of weightings between the hedged item and the hedging instrument to achieve an accounting outcome that is inconsistent with the purpose of hedge accounting. It does not imply that the hedge relationship must be perfect, but only that the weightings of the hedging instrument and hedged item actually used are not selected to introduce or to avoid accounting ineffectiveness.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.4.10].

<a id="s297-Para_46.49"></a>**46.49** The entity should document how it has determined the hedge ratio and how this complies with its risk management strategy and hedging objective. When determining the appropriate hedge ratio, management would take into consideration, among other things, the following factors:

- availability of hedging instruments and nature of the underlying of those hedging instruments (and, as a consequence, the level of the risk of differences in value changes involved between the hedged item and the hedging instrument);

- tolerance levels in relation to expected sources of hedge ineffectiveness (which determine when the hedging relationship is adjusted for risk management purposes); and

- costs of hedging (including the costs of adjusting an existing hedging relationship).

[IFRS 9 para BC6.243]. [IFRS 9 para 6.4.1(b)].

[FAQ 46.49.1 – How is the hedge ratio determined? ](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.49.1)  
[FAQ 46.49.2 – In which circumstances might an entity designate a hedge ratio that could create ineffectiveness?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.49.2)  
[FAQ 46.49.3 – How should an entity select the appropriate hedge ratio?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.49.3)

**d) Rebalancing**

<a id="s297-Para_46.50"></a>**46.50** IFRS 9 introduces the concept of ‘rebalancing’. Rebalancing refers to adjustments to the designated quantities of either the hedged item or the hedging instrument of an existing hedging relationship for the purpose of maintaining a hedge ratio that complies with the hedge effectiveness requirements. This allows entities to respond to changes that arise in the relationship between the underlying or risk variables. Rebalancing does not result in de-designation and re-designation of the hedge. It is accounted for as a continuation of the hedging relationship that, in many cases, will reduce the amount of ineffectiveness reported in future periods as compared to de-designating and re-designating the hedge. However, on rebalancing, hedge ineffectiveness is determined and recognised immediately before adjusting the hedge relationship.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.7]. [IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.8]. [IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.9].

<a id="s297-Para_46.51"></a>**46.51** Rebalancing is consistent with the requirement of avoiding an imbalance in the weightings between the hedged item and the hedging instrument, not only at inception of the hedge but also at each reporting date and on any significant change in circumstances. Entities should not need to rebalance very often if they have a good risk management strategy in place and the economic relationship is stable.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.4.10].

<a id="s297-Para_46.52"></a>**46.52** An entity must update its documentation of the analysis of the sources of hedge ineffectiveness that are expected to affect the hedging relationship during its remaining term, when rebalancing a hedging relationship.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.21].

<a id="s297-Para_46.53"></a>**46.53** Rebalancing is not always applicable. An example is where the changes in the hedge relationship – which might arise from changes in the derivative counterparty credit risk – cannot be compensated by adjusting the hedge ratio. Rebalancing is also not allowed if the risk management objective has changed. Hedge accounting should be discontinued.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.15].

[FAQ 46.53.1 – What is a rebalancing and what are the accounting consequences?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.53.1)  
[FAQ 46.53.2 – Should the terms of a hypothetical derivative be reset to have a zero fair value after rebalancing?](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.53.2)

**e) Discounted cash flows for measuring hedge ineffectiveness**

<a id="s297-Para_46.54"></a>**46.54** Hedge accounting does not change the measurement of the hedging instrument, it only affects where the change in its carrying amount is presented. Hedging instruments are subject to measurement at either fair value or amortised cost, both of which take into consideration the time value of money.

[IFRS 9 para BC6.281].

<a id="s297-Para_46.55"></a>**46.55** The hedged item is also measured on a present value basis. Subsequent changes would include the effect of the time value of money; for example, an undiscounted spot approach cannot be used in a foreign currency hedge. This requirement ensures that the measurement of the effectiveness of the hedge relationship reflects the time value of money, and that any mismatches in timing between the hedged item and the hedging instrument are recognised as ineffectiveness. See also [paragraph 46.76](74.md#s298-Para_46.76) for information on partial term hedges.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.4].

<a id="s298-chap_46_navPoint_12"></a>
## Eligible hedged items (paras 46.56- 46.96)

<a id="s298-chap_46_navPoint_13"></a>
### Definition

 

<a id="s298-Para_46.56"></a>**46.56** A hedged item can be a recognised asset or liability, unrecognised firm commitment, highly probable forecast transaction, or net investment in a foreign operation. The hedged item can be a single item or a group of items, but it must be reliably measurable.

[IFRS 9 para 6.3.1]. [IFRS 9 para 6.3.2]. [IFRS 9 para 6.3.3].

<a id="s298-Para_46.57"></a>**46.57** A hedged item must normally expose the entity to risk of changes in the fair value or future cash flows that could affect P&amp;L. This could be in the current or future periods. Hedge accounting cannot apply to any items included in equity or transactions that directly affect equity, except for equity instruments for which an entity has elected to present changes in fair value in OCI. Such an equity instrument can be a hedged item in a fair value hedge, provided the hedged exposure is one that could affect OCI. In that case, any hedge ineffectiveness is also recognised in OCI.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.2]. [IFRS 9 para 6.5.3].

[FAQ 46.57.1 – Is a highly probable forecast foreign currency debt issuance eligible as a hedged item in a cash flow hedge of foreign currency risk?](76.md#s304-FAQ_46.57.1)  
[FAQ 46.57.2 – Can the parent apply hedge accounting to a forecast capital contribution in its separate financial statements?](76.md#s304-FAQ_46.57.2)

 

<a id="s298-chap_46_navPoint_14"></a>
### Designation of groups of items as hedged items

 

For group hedges (including macro hedges) impacted by IBOR reform, where the retrospective effectiveness assessment relief set out in Phase 1 Interest Rate Benchmark Reform Amendments to IFRS 9, IAS 39 and IFRS 7 was applied, refer to [Appendix 3  para A3.32](88.md#s376-Para_A3.32) for further information. Refer to [Appendix 3 para A3.41](88.md#s379-Para_A3.41) for the Phase 2 amendments issued in August 2020.

  

<a id="s298-Para_46.58"></a>**46.58** Items (including net positions) can be grouped together and designated as a hedged item, if:

- the items are individually eligible hedged items; and

- the items are managed together for risk management purposes.

[IFRS 9 para 6.6.1].

It is not necessary for each item in the group to share all of the same risks, as long as the items are individually eligible and they are managed together on a group basis for risk management purposes.

[IFRS 9 para 6.6.1(a), (b)].

 

<a id="s298-chap_46_navPoint_15"></a>
### Hedging an overall net position

 

<a id="s298-Para_46.59"></a>**46.59** An entity can designate an overall net position as the hedged item, provided it is managed as a net position for risk management purposes. However, it must designate the gross amounts within the net position, and not a non-specific abstract amount.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.6.1]. [IFRS 9 App B para B6.6.4].

[FAQ 46.59.1 – In what situation can an entity hedge a net position?](76.md#s304-FAQ_46.59.1)

<a id="s298-Para_46.60"></a>**46.60** There are additional qualifying criteria for cash flow hedges of a group of items that are expected to affect P&amp;L in different reporting periods:

- Only hedges of foreign currency risk are allowed.

- The items within the net position must be specified in such a way that the pattern of how they will affect P&amp;L is set out as part of the initial hedge designation and documentation (this should include at least the reporting period, nature and volume).

[IFRS 9 para 6.6.1(c)].

<a id="s298-Para_46.61"></a>**46.61** Hedges of a net position are permitted, but the gains and losses on recycling are presented as a separate line item in P&amp;L or OCI (separate from the hedged items). An entity cannot present the post-hedging results of its commercial activities for the individual line items affected. See [EX 46.100.3](76.md#s304-EX46.100.3) for an example of hedging a net position in a group situation.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.6.15].

<a id="s298-Para_46.62"></a>**46.62** For cash flow hedges of a group of items with no offsetting risk position, the presentation of gains and losses should be apportioned to the line items affected by the hedged items.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.6.14].

<a id="s298-Para_46.63"></a>**46.63** Net nil positions, where hedged items among themselves fully offset the risk that is managed on a group basis, are also allowed to be designated in a hedging relationship that does not include a hedging instrument, provided that all of the following criteria are met:

- the hedge is part of a rolling net risk hedging strategy (that is, the entity routinely hedges new positions of the same type);

- the hedged net position changes in size over the life, and the entity uses eligible hedging instruments to hedge the net risk;

- hedge accounting is normally applied to such net positions; and

- not applying hedge accounting to the net nil position would give rise to inconsistent accounting outcomes.

Net nil positions are expected to be rare in practice.

[IFRS 9 para 6.6.6].

<a id="s298-Para_46.63.1"></a>**46.63.1**IFRS 9 also allows an entity to designate some of the underlying gross items as the hedged item equal in amount to the net position. For example, an entity (C functional currency) with a firm commitment to make a purchase in a foreign currency of FC100 and a firm commitment to make a sale in the foreign currency of FC90 can hedge the net amount of FC10 by acquiring a derivative and designating it as a hedging instrument associated with FC10 of the firm purchase commitment of FC100. Similarly, an entity with C100 of fixed-rate assets and C90 of fixed-rate liabilities with terms of a similar nature could hedge the net C10 exposure by designating C10 of those assets as the hedged item.

[IFRS 9 para BC6.96]. [IFRS 9 para BC6.97]. [IFRS 9 para BC6.98]. [IFRS 9 para BC6.99]. [IFRS 9 para BC6.100]. [IFRS 9 para BC6.101].

### Components

 

<a id="s298-Para_46.65"></a>**46.65** An entity can designate an item in its entirety as a hedged item, or it can designate a component of such an item. Permitted components are:

- changes in the cash flows or fair value of an item attributable to a specific risk or risks (a risk component), provided that the risk component is separately identifiable and reliably measurable (see [para 46.66](74.md#s298-Para_46.66) onwards);

- one or more selected contractual cash flows; and

- components of a nominal amount (that is, a specified part of the amount of an item).

[IFRS 9 para 6.3.7].

 

<a id="s298-chap_46_navPoint_idp1688649560"></a>
#### Risk components

 

<a id="s298-Para_46.66"></a>**46.66** Risk components of financial and non-financial items (such as the benchmark interest rate in a loan that bears interest at a floating benchmark interest rate plus a spread, or the coffee price that reflects benchmark quality on a coffee supply contract) can be designated as a hedged item, provided they are separately identifiable and reliably measurable. This requirement could be met where the risk component is either explicitly stated in a contract (contractually specified) or implicit in the fair value or cash flows (non-contractually specified) – see [paragraph 46.71](74.md#s298-Para_46.71) onwards for further details of how this might occur in practice. Permitted risk components include designation of only changes in the cash flows, or the fair value of a hedged item above or below a specified price or other variable (a one-sided risk).

[IFRS 9 para 6.3.7(a)]. [IFRS 9 App B para 6.3.8]. [IFRS 9 App B para 6.3.10]. [IFRS 9 App B para B6.3.12].

###### Ineligible risk components

 

<a id="s298-Para_46.74"></a>**46.74**There is a rebuttable presumption that, unless inflation risk is contractually specified, it is not separately identifiable and reliably measurable. Therefore, it cannot be designated as a risk component of a financial instrument such as a fixed-rate debt instrument. #### Hedging layers of a group

 

<a id="s298-Para_46.77"></a>**46.77** A layer of a group can be designated as the hedged item. A layer component can be specified from a defined, but open, population or from a defined nominal amount. Examples include:

- a part of a monetary transaction volume (such as the next C10 cash flows from sales denominated in a foreign currency after the first C20 in March 201X);

- a part of a physical or other transaction volume (such as the first 100 barrels of the oil purchases in June 201X, or the first 100 MWh of electricity sales in June 201X); or

- a layer of the nominal amount of the hedged item (such as the last C80 million of a C100 million firm commitment, the bottom layer of C20 million of a C100 million fixed-rate bond or the top layer of C30 million from a total amount of C100 million of a fixed-rate debt that can be prepaid, where the defined nominal amount is C100 million).

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.3.18].

<a id="s298-Para_46.78"></a>**46.78**If a layer component is designated in a fair value hedge, an entity must specify it from a defined nominal amount. [IFRS 9 App B para B6.3.20].

### Firm commitments

 

<a id="s298-Para_46.80"></a>**46.80** A firm commitment is a binding agreement for the exchange of a specified quantity of resources at a specified price on a specified future date or dates. Firm commitments are most commonly for the delivery of physical goods (such as a commodity, inventory or property, plant and equipment), but they can also be for services, intangible assets or financial instruments (although the latter would likely be a derivative instrument, in which case it can be designated as a hedged item only in very limited circumstances – see paras [46.94](74.md#s298-Para_46.94) to [46.95](74.md#s298-Para_46.95)). The key characteristic of a firm commitment is that it must have specified terms, namely specified quantity, price and timing of the transaction. A contract with fully flexible pricing, timing and quantities would always be settled at fair value on the day, so it would not be eligible for fair value hedge accounting – there must be some fair value exposure. However, a contract with a pricing formula that is part fixed and part variable does meet the definition of a firm commitment if all terms are specified. The entity could therefore choose to hedge a component of that firm commitment (see [para 46.65 onwards](74.md#s298-Para_46.65)). This would limit the ineffectiveness suffered by entities wishing to hedge risks on non-financial items. A firm commitment must be with a party that is external to the entity ([see para 46.84](74.md#s298-Para_46.84)). A firm commitment is usually legally enforceable.

[IFRS 9 App A Defined terms].

<a id="s298-Para_46.81"></a>**46.81**Hedges of firm commitments are generally treated as fair value hedges, except in one situation. The hedge of the foreign currency risk in a firm commitment can be accounted for as a cash flow hedge of the foreign currency risk. If a firm commitment has a price specified in the functional currency of the entity, rather than a price specified in a foreign currency, there would be no cash flow variability in the entity’s functional currency in the anticipated transaction, and cash flow hedging of foreign currency risk would not be possible. For this reason, hedges of firm commitments, other than those denominated in a foreign currency, are generally accounted for as fair value hedges, because the entity is exposed to changes in fair value of that commitment.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.4].

[FAQ 46.81.1 – Can cash flows expected to occur as a result of entering into a firm commitment be designated in a cash flow hedge?](76.md#s304-FAQ_46.81.1)

<a id="s298-Para_46.82"></a>**46.82** Where an unrecognised firm commitment to acquire an asset or assume a liability is designated as a hedged item in a fair value hedge, the accounting treatment is described in [paragraph 46.11](74.md#s296-Para_46.11).

[FAQ 46.82.1 – How should an entity measure and recognise the fair value of a firm commitment designated in a fair value hedge?](76.md#s304-FAQ_46.82.1)

<a id="s298-chap_46_navPoint_20"></a>
### Intra-group and intra-entity hedging transactions

 

<a id="s298-Para_46.84"></a>**46.84** Group entities commonly transact with other group members or segments. These transactions might expose the entities to risks that they wish to hedge. The entity exposed to the risk can hedge it by entering into internal derivative contracts with, say, group treasury, or through external derivative contracts. The general rule is that only assets, liabilities, firm commitments or highly probable forecast transactions that involve a party external to the entity can be designated as hedged items. Such intra-group transactions can be designated as hedged items only in the individual or separate financial statements of those entities, and not in the consolidated financial statements of the reporting group, except for transactions between an investment entity and its subsidiaries measured at FVTPL in the investment entity’s consolidated accounts. Intra-group transactions eliminate on consolidation and do not expose the consolidated group to any risk that affects consolidated P&amp;L.

[IFRS 9 para 6.3.5].

<a id="s298-Para_46.85"></a>**46.85** There are, however, two exceptions to this general rule involving foreign currency exposures:

- foreign exchange gains and losses on intra-group monetary items; and

- forecast intra-group transactions.

[IFRS 9 para 6.3.6].

<a id="s298-Para_46.86"></a>**46.86** Under IAS 21, foreign exchange gains and losses on an intra-group monetary asset (or liability) between group entities with different functional currencies, whether short-term or long-term, do not fully eliminate in the consolidated P&amp;L. A foreign currency monetary item represents a commitment to convert one currency into another and exposes the reporting entity to a gain or loss through currency fluctuations. Accordingly, in the reporting entity’s consolidated financial statements, such exchange differences continue to be recognised in P&amp;L. The foreign currency exposure on such an intra-group monetary item can be designated as a hedged item on consolidation.

[IFRS 9 para 6.3.6].

[FAQ 46.86.1 – How can a parent, in its consolidated financial statements, hedge the foreign currency exposure of an intra-group receivable?](76.md#s304-FAQ_46.86.1)  
[FAQ 46.86.2 – Can a group, in its consolidated financial statements, hedge the foreign currency and interest rate exposure of an external borrowing that is on-lent to a subsidiary with a different functional currency?](76.md#s304-FAQ_46.86.2)

<a id="s298-Para_46.87"></a>**46.87** The foreign currency risk of a highly probable forecast intra-group transaction could be designated as a hedged item in the consolidated financial statements, provided the following two conditions are met:

- the highly probable forecast intra-group transaction is denominated in a currency other than the functional currency of the group member entering into that transaction; and

- the foreign currency risk will affect the group’s consolidated P&amp;L.

[IFRS 9 para 6.3.6].

<a id="s298-Para_46.88"></a>**46.88**The group member entering into the transaction can be a parent, subsidiary, associate, joint arrangement or branch. The first condition (that is, that the intra-group transaction is denominated in a currency other than the functional currency of the group member entering into such transaction) is necessary because, under IAS 21, a foreign currency exposure arises only where a transaction is denominated in a currency other than the functional currency of the entity entering into that transaction. The second condition (that is, that the foreign currency risk will affect the group’s consolidated P&amp;L) is met if the forecast intra-group transaction is related to an external transaction. An example is forecast sales or purchases of inventories between members of the same group if there is an onward sale of the inventory to a party external to the group. However, if there is no external related transaction (which is usually the case for royalty payments, interest payments or management charges between members of the same group), the foreign currency risk of those forecast intra-group transactions would not affect consolidated P&amp;L, and so cannot qualify as hedged items. Forecast intra-group foreign currency dividends can never qualify as hedged items ([see para 46.91](74.md#s298-Para_46.91)).

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.3.5].

[FAQ 46.88.1 – Can hedge accounting be applied to all highly probable forecast inter-company transactions?](76.md#s304-FAQ_46.88.1)

<a id="s298-Para_46.89"></a>**46.89** If a hedge of a forecast intra-group transaction qualifies for hedge accounting, any gain or loss that is recognised directly in equity should be reclassified into P&amp;L in the same period or periods during which the foreign currency risk of the hedged transaction affects consolidated P&amp;L.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.3.6].

[FAQ 46.89.1 – How can an entity demonstrate linkage to an external transaction in order to hedge the foreign currency risk of an intra-group forecast transaction?](76.md#s304-FAQ_46.89.1)

 

<a id="s298-chap_46_navPoint_21"></a>
### Items that do not qualify as hedged items

<a id="s298-Para_46.90"></a>**46.90** There are a number of items that, for various reasons, cannot qualify as a hedged item in a hedging relationship. For such items, the normal measurement and recognition rules apply.

<a id="s298-chap_46_navPoint_idp1714485096"></a>
#### Own equity instruments

 

<a id="s298-Para_46.91"></a>**46.91** Hedge accounting cannot be applied for hedges of any items included in equity or transactions that directly affect equity. Such items do not create an exposure to risk that will affect P&amp;L or OCI. Therefore, hedges of risks relating to the forecast sale, purchase or redemption of an entity’s own equity shares cannot qualify as hedged items. Similarly, distributions to holders of an equity instrument are debited by the issuer directly to equity. Therefore, such distributions cannot be designated as a hedged item. However, a dividend that has been approved in a general meeting of members, has not yet been paid and is recognised as a financial liability might qualify as a hedged item (for example, for foreign currency risk if it is denominated in a foreign currency).

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.1]. [IAS 32 para 25].

[FAQ 46.91.1 – Are forecast inter-company dividends an eligible hedged item in consolidated financial statements?](76.md#s304-FAQ_46.91.1)  
[FAQ 46.91.2 – Can a forecast transaction in an entity’s own shares be designated as the hedged item?](76.md#s304-FAQ_46.91.2)

 

<a id="s298-chap_46_navPoint_idp1714494568"></a>
#### Equity method investment and investment in consolidated subsidiaries

 

<a id="s298-Para_46.92"></a>**46.92**An equity method investment, such as an associate or joint arrangement, cannot be a hedged item in a fair value hedge. The equity method recognises in P&amp;L the investor’s share of the associate’s profit or loss, rather than changes in the investment’s fair value in the consolidated financial statements. However, an entity might be able to designate an investment in an associate, joint arrangement or subsidiary as a fair value hedge in its separate financial statements, provided that its fair value can be measured reliably.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.3.2].

<a id="s298-Para_46.93"></a>**46.93** An investment in a consolidated subsidiary cannot be a hedged item in a fair value hedge in the consolidated accounts. Consolidation recognises in P&amp;L the subsidiary’s profit or loss, rather than changes in the investment’s fair value. If a subsidiary was allowed to be designated as a hedged item, it would result in double counting, as both the income from the investment as well as changes in the fair value would be reported in P&amp;L. A hedge of a net investment in a foreign operation is different, because it is a hedge of the foreign currency exposure, and not a fair value hedge of the change in the investment’s value.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.3.2].

#### Derivative instruments

 

<a id="s298-Para_46.94"></a>**46.94** A derivative instrument (whether a stand-alone or separately recognised embedded derivative) can only be designated as a hedged item as part of an aggregated exposure with a non-derivative item ([see para 46.64)](74.md#s298-Para_46.64). As an exception, a purchased option could be hedged by a written option (for example, a written call option used to hedge a callable liability).

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.2.4].

<a id="s298-Para_46.95"></a>**46.95** A contract to buy or sell a non-financial asset that can be 'settled net' in cash will be accounted for as a derivative under IFRS 9, unless it meets the ‘own use’ exception (discussed in [chapter 40 para 81](61.md#s252-Para_40.81)) and so cannot be a hedged item. This is also the case if the entity has applied the fair value option to contracts that do qualify for ‘own use’ ([see further para 46.155](74.md#s301-Para_46.155)). In March 2019, the IFRS IC issued an agenda decision ‘Physical settlement of Contracts to Buy or Sell a Non-financial Item’. This addressed the accounting treatment where a contract to buy or sell a non-financial asset that is within the scope of IFRS 9 (and measured at FVTPL) is settled by physical delivery of the non-financial asset, resulting in the recognition of revenue or inventory. The issue was at what amount should revenue or inventory be recognised and whether this should include the previously recognised fair value gain or loss on the contract (that would result in the recognition of revenue or inventory at the contract price). The IFRS IC concluded that it should not. It noted that a journal entry to reverse the accumulated fair value gain or loss on a contract that does not meet the ‘own use’ scope exception and is measured at FVTPL under IFRS 9 is neither permitted nor required. The IFRS IC also noted that there is no gain or loss on the derivative caused by settlement. See chapter 4, [FAQ 4.114.4](10.md#s30-FAQ_4.114.4) for further details on presentation of failed ‘own use’ contracts. However, such a contract might be able to be designated as an all-in-one hedge, in which case the effect would be different. See [FAQ 46.133.1](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.133.1) for further guidance on ‘all-in-one’ hedges.

 

<a id="s298-chap_46_navPoint_idp1714519272"></a>
#### General business risk

<a id="s298-Para_46.96"></a>**46.96** A hedge must relate to a specific identified and designated risk and not merely to the entity’s general business risks, and it must ultimately affect the entity’s P&amp;L to qualify for hedge accounting. For example, a firm commitment to acquire a business in a business combination cannot be a hedged item, except for foreign currency risk, because the other risks being hedged cannot be specifically identified and measured; these other risks are general business risks. Similarly, the risk that a transaction will not occur, such that it would result in less revenue than expected, is an overall business risk that is not eligible as a hedged item. A hedge of the risk of obsolescence of a physical asset, or the risk of expropriation of property by a government, is also not eligible for hedge accounting; effectiveness cannot be measured, because those risks are not reliably measurable.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.3.1].

## Eligible hedging instruments (paras 46.97-46.144)

 

<a id="s299-Para_46.97"></a>**46.97** The main qualifying hedging instruments are:

- derivative instruments, including separate embedded derivatives in financial liabilities or non-financial contracts;

- non-derivative financial instruments measured at fair value through P&amp;L (FVTPL) (except for financial liabilities designated at FVTPL where changes in fair value as a result of credit risk are presented in OCI); and

- the foreign currency component of non-derivative financial instruments (except for equity instruments for which changes in fair value are presented in OCI).

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.1]. [IFRS 9 para 6.2.2]. [IFRS 9 para 6.2.3].

<a id="s299-Para_46.98"></a>**46.98** Any combination of derivatives (or a proportion of them) and non-derivatives (or a proportion of them) can be jointly designated as a hedging instrument. See further [paragraph 46.130](74.md#s299-Para_46.130) onwards.

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.5].

<a id="s299-Para_46.99"></a>**46.99** Most derivative financial instruments (except for some written options) can be designated as hedging instruments ([see para 46.102](74.md#s299-Para_46.102)), provided they are entered into with an external party. Intra-group derivatives and other contracts do not qualify as hedging instruments in consolidated financial statements, although they might qualify in the separate financial statements of individual entities in the group or for segmental reporting, provided the information reported to the chief operating decision maker (CODM) is on that basis. [IFRS 9 para 6.2.3]. [IFRIC 16 para 14].

### Portions and proportions of a hedging instrument

 

<a id="s299-Para_46.106"></a>**46.106** A derivative cannot be designated as a hedging instrument for a part of its changes in fair value that result from only a portion of the remaining life of the derivative (such as the next three years of a swap with a remaining life of five years). But it is possible to designate a proportion of the entire instrument, such as 50% of the nominal amount. The proportion that is not designated as a hedge can be designated as a hedging instrument in another hedge relationship; otherwise, it will be measured at FVTPL.

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.4(c)].

[FAQ 46.106.1 – Is it possible to use proportions of derivatives as hedging instruments?](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.106.1)

<a id="s299-Para_46.107"></a>**46.107** There is normally a single fair value measure for a hedging instrument in its entirety, and the factors that cause changes in fair value are co-dependent. Thus, a hedging relationship is designated by an entity for a hedging instrument in its entirety. It follows that a derivative instrument cannot be split into components representing different risks, with only certain components designated as a hedging instrument. The only exceptions permitted are:

- separating the forward element and spot element of a forward contract and designating only the change in the spot element as the hedging instrument;

- separating and excluding the foreign currency basis spread from the designated hedging instrument; and

- separating the intrinsic value and time value of an option contract and designating only the change in intrinsic value of an option as the hedging instrument.

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.4].

<a id="s299-Para_46.108"></a>**46.108** These exceptions are permitted because the separate elements can generally be measured separately. Furthermore, all of the forward element of a forward contract, cross-currency basis spreads and the time value of an option are seen by IFRS 9 as a ‘cost of hedging’, that is, a cost of achieving the risk mitigation inherent in the hedge. In line with this ‘cost of hedging’ rationale, the timing of the related cost in P&amp;L is matched to that of the item being hedged. This is described in more detail in [paragraph 46.117](74.md#s299-Para_46.117) onwards.

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.4].

<a id="s299-chap_46_navPoint_27"></a>
### Hedging with options

 

<a id="s299-Para_46.109"></a>**46.109** Options, in contrast to forward contracts and swaps, give the holder the right but not the obligation to exercise the instrument and exchange the underlyings. A purchased option has potential gains equal to or greater than losses, so can reduce P&amp;L volatility from changes in fair values or cash flows. As it has the potential to reduce risk exposure, it can qualify as a hedging instrument.

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.1].

<a id="s299-Para_46.110"></a>**46.110** An entity can designate as the hedged item only changes in the cash flows or fair value above or below a specified price or other variable (a ‘one-sided risk’). In a hedge of one-sided risk, entities are permitted to designate the hedged risk as the intrinsic value of a purchased option that has the same principal terms as the hedged item, but cannot include the time value of the option. For example, an entity can designate the variability of future cash flow outcomes resulting from a price increase of a forecast commodity purchase. Only cash flow losses that result from an increase in the price above the specified level are designated. The inclusion of an option’s time value is prohibited in the designated one-sided risk on a non-optional hedged item, because time value is not a component of a forecast transaction that will affect P&amp;L. Greater hedge effectiveness is, therefore, likely to be achieved if the hedging instrument is designated in the same way – that is, to exclude time value ([see para 46.118](74.md#s299-Para_46.118)).

[IFRS 9 para 6.3.7]. [IFRS 9 App B para B6.3.12].

[FAQ 46.110.1 – Can an entity use a purchased option as a hedging instrument in a cash flow hedge?](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.110.1)

 

<a id="s299-chap_46_navPoint_idp1688669400"></a>
#### Written options

 

<a id="s299-Para_46.111"></a>**46.111** A written option (as opposed to a purchased option) exposes its writer to the possibility of unlimited loss, but it limits the gain to the amount of premium received. A written option is not effective in reducing the P&amp;L exposure of a hedged item. Therefore, a written option does not qualify as a hedging instrument on its own, unless it is designated as an offset to a purchased option. A written option can also be designated as an offset to a purchased option that is embedded in another financial instrument. For example, in a callable debt that allows the issuer of the debt to repay it early at a fixed amount, the issuer can designate a written option as a hedging instrument of the call feature embedded in the debt.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.2.4].

<a id="s299-Para_46.112"></a>**46.112** Nevertheless, a written option might be an eligible hedging instrument when combined with other purchased options (regardless of whether they arise from one or several different contracts), provided that the combination does not represent a net written option (for which a net premium is received). For example, an entity’s hedging strategy might involve entering into a combination of a written put option and a purchased call option to form a collar. The objective is to protect the entity from loss below a certain value (the floor/written put) and limit the upside potential for gain above a certain value (the cap/purchased call), so as to reduce the cost of the hedging strategy. A typical example is an interest rate collar.

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.6].

<a id="s299-Para_46.113"></a>**46.113** An interest rate collar might be designated as a hedging instrument, if the combination is a net purchased option or zero cost collar (that is, it is neither a net written nor a net purchased option). Typically, such an instrument would not be a net written option, provided that:

- No net premium is received, either at inception or over the life of the combination of options. The distinguishing feature of a written option is the receipt of a premium to compensate the writer for the risk incurred.

- Except for the strike prices, the critical terms and conditions of the written option component and the purchased option component are the same (including underlying variable or variables, currency denomination and maturity date).

- The notional amount of the written option component is not greater than the notional amount of the purchased option component.

[IFRS 9 para BC6.153].

<a id="s299-Para_46.114"></a>**46.114** Where a collar whose fair value was zero at inception is designated as a hedging instrument some time into its life, it might have some fair value on the date of designation. If its fair value on the date of designation is negative, the first condition in [paragraph 46.113](74.md#s299-Para_46.113) would be failed, and such a collar would not be allowed for designation as a hedging instrument. However, if the negative fair value is only due to the deferral of a premium, then the negative nature does not create an exposure linked to net written options, and therefore the instrument could be designated as a hedging instrument providing the other conditions are met. If the collar’s fair value is zero or positive, it could be designated as a hedging instrument (subject to compliance with the two other conditions).

[IFRS 9 para BC6.153].

<a id="s299-Para_46.115"></a>**46.115** If a combination of a written option and a purchased option (such as an interest rate collar) is transacted as a single instrument with one counterparty, an entity cannot split the derivative instrument into its written option component and purchased option component and designate the purchased option component as a hedging instrument. This is because a hedging relationship is designated by an entity for a hedging instrument in its entirety, and the remaining written option is not, on its own, an eligible hedging instrument.

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.6]. [IFRS 9 para BC6.153].

#### Intrinsic value

 

<a id="s299-Para_46.116"></a>**46.116** The fair value of an option can be divided into two portions: the intrinsic value, which is the difference between the strike price and current market price of the option’s underlying; and the time value, which is the option’s remaining value and depends on the volatility of the price of the underlying, interest rates and the time remaining to maturity. Changes in the option’s time value will not be offset by an equivalent change in the value or cash flows of the hedged item.

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.4(a)].

<a id="s299-chap_46_navPoint_idp1688707160"></a>
#### Separating the intrinsic and time value of an option

 

<a id="s299-Para_46.117"></a>**46.117** IFRS 9 introduces the concept of ‘cost of hedging’. Where a purchased option is designated as a hedging instrument, IFRS 9 views the option as similar to purchasing insurance cover, with the time value being the associated cost.

[IFRS 9 para BC6.299].

<a id="s299-Para_46.118"></a>**46.118**Many entities might choose to designate only the intrinsic value of an option as the hedging instrument, particularly (as described in[ para 46.110](74.md#s299-Para_46.110)) where the hedged item is a one-sided risk and so the time value cannot be designated as part of the hedged risk. However, in a more dynamic hedging model, an entity might choose to designate all fair value changes in the hedging relationship.

<a id="s299-Para_46.119"></a>**46.119**If an entity elects to designate only the intrinsic value of the option as the hedging instrument, it must account for the changes in the aligned time value ([see para 46.121](74.md#s299-Para_46.121)) in OCI and hold them in a hedging reserve in equity, regardless of whether the hedge is a cash flow or fair value hedge. The subsequent release of the time value of the option depends on whether the hedge is transaction related or time-period related:

- For a transaction-related hedge that results in the recognition of, or a firm commitment for, a non-financial asset or non-financial liability, the time value of the option is removed from equity and recorded in the initial cost or other carrying amount of the asset or liability. This is not a reclassification adjustment and does not affect other comprehensive income.

- For other transaction-related hedges, the time value of the option is reclassified from equity as a reclassification adjustment to profit or loss in the same period or periods that the hedged cash flows affect profit or loss. Any amount that is not expected to be recovered in one or more future periods should be immediately reclassified into profit and loss.

- For time-period-related hedges, the time value of the option is amortised on a systematic and rational basis over the period during which the option’s intrinsic value could affect profit or loss (or other comprehensive income where the hedged item is an equity instrument whose fair value is recorded through other comprehensive income). This is recorded as a reclassification adjustment.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.15].

[EX 46.119.2 – Example of separating the intrinsic and time value of the option, where the hedged item is a firm commitment to purchase a non-financial item (transaction related)](77.md#s305-EX46.119.2)  
[EX 46.119.3 – Example of separating the intrinsic and time value of the option, where the hedged item is a fair value hedge of inventory (time-period related)](77.md#s305-EX46.119.3)

[FAQ 46.35.8 – Can a trader achieve hedge accounting for a cash and carry arbitrage trade if this is in line with its risk management strategy? (demonstrates cost of hedging model)](75.md#s303-FAQ_46.35.8)

<a id="s299-Para_46.120"></a>**46.120**Once an entity designates the intrinsic value of the option, the accounting in IFRS 9 is not optional, but mandatory. However, this accounting limits P&amp;L volatility for option-based hedges. It removes an obstacle to sensible risk management practice.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.15].

<a id="s299-Para_46.121"></a>**46.121**The accounting for the initial time value of purchased options applies only to the extent that the time value relates to the hedged item. This is called the ‘aligned time value’. Where the hedging instrument and hedged item are not fully aligned, entities need to determine the aligned time value – that is, how much of the time value included in the premium paid (actual time value) relates to the hedged item – and apply the accounting treatment to that portion. This can be determined using the valuation of an option that would have critical terms that perfectly match the hedged item.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.32].

<a id="s299-Para_46.122"></a>**46.122** If the actual time value is higher than the aligned time value at inception, the amount recorded in a separate component of equity is based on the aligned time value. Differences in the fair value changes between the two are recorded in P&amp;L. If the actual time value is lower at inception, the amount to be recorded in equity is the lower of the cumulative change in fair value of the actual and aligned time values. The residual amount is recognised in P&amp;L. It might be difficult to obtain the ‘perfect’ option, and so entities will need to address these accounting complexities.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.33].

### Hedging with forward contracts

<a id="s299-Para_46.123"></a>**46.123** A forward is a contract to exchange a fixed amount of a financial or non-financial asset on a fixed future date at a fixed price. The use of forward contracts as hedging instruments is a common hedging strategy. The first requirement is to decide whether the hedging instrument can be accounted for as a forward contract.

[FAQ 46.123.1 – What is a forward contract?](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.123.1)

<a id="s299-chap_46_navPoint_idp1688739160"></a>
#### Separating the forward and spot elements of a forward contract

<a id="s299-Para_46.124"></a>**46.124** The fair value of a forward contract is affected by changes in the spot rate and changes in the forward points. In the case of a foreign currency forward contract, the forward points arise from the interest rate differential between currencies specified in a forward contract.

[IFRS 9 App B para BC6.416].

<a id="s299-Para_46.125"></a>**46.125** Management might want to exclude the forward points from the hedge relationship in order to improve effectiveness. Unlike the accounting for options, where it is mandatory to take movements in time value to OCI and equity if only the intrinsic value has been designated as a hedging instrument, the hedge accounting treatment for forward points is optional.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.16].

[FAQ 46.125.1 – What approaches are available for entities hedging foreign currency using a forward contract?](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.125.1)

<a id="s299-Para_46.126"></a>**46.126** Where the spot rate is the designated hedged risk, entities will still need to consider the time value of money and measure the hedged item using discounted spot (as discussed at [paras 46.54](74.md#s297-Para_46.54) to [46.55](74.md#s297-Para_46.55)). For a short-term hedge between currencies with limited interest rate volatility, the impact might not be significant.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.4].

<a id="s299-chap_46_navPoint_29"></a>
### Accounting for currency basis spreads

 

<a id="s299-Para_46.127"></a>**46.127** The hypothetical derivative method is a common way of assessing whether a hedge meets the hedge effectiveness requirements. A hypothetical derivative cannot include features that do not exist in the hedged item. A hypothetical derivative cannot simply impute a charge for exchanging different currencies (that is, the currency basis spread), even though actual derivatives under which different currencies are exchanged might include such a charge (for example, cross-currency interest rate swaps).

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.5].

<a id="s299-Para_46.128"></a>**46.128** Where an entity separates the foreign currency basis spread from a financial instrument and excludes it from the designation of that financial instrument as the hedging instrument, the entity is able to account for the changes in the currency basis spread in the same manner as could be applied to the forward element of a forward contract (as noted in [para 46.124 onwards](74.md#s299-Para_46.124)). This treatment is optional but, if adopted, will help to mitigate volatility in P&amp;L.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.39]. [IFRS 9 para 6.2.4(b)].

<a id="s299-chap_46_navPoint_30"></a>
### Hedging more than one risk with a single instrument

 

<a id="s299-Para_46.129"></a>**46.129**A derivative, such as a forward contract, a swap or an option, is used as a hedging instrument to hedge a single risk (foreign currency, interest rate or equity price risk). However, an entity might use a single derivative such as a cross-currency swap (combined interest rate and currency swap) to convert a variable-rate position in a foreign currency to a fixed-rate position in the entity’s functional currency. A single hedging instrument could be designated as a hedge of more than one type of risk, provided there is specific designation of the hedging instrument and of the different risk positions as hedged items. The hedged items can be in different hedging relationships.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.2.6].

<a id="s299-chap_46_navPoint_31"></a>
### Combination of hedging instruments

 

<a id="s299-Para_46.130"></a>**46.130** A derivative could also be designated as the hedging instrument in combination with one or more other derivatives, or in combination with groups of non-derivative assets or liabilities. Two or more derivatives (or proportions of them) could be viewed in combination and jointly designated as the hedging instrument, provided together they do not create a net written option (see further [paras 46.111 to 46.115](74.md#s299-Para_46.111)).

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.5].

<a id="s299-Para_46.131"></a>**46.131** Derivative instruments do not have to be similar to be combined, either together or with non-derivative instruments. Even where the risks arising from some derivatives offset those arising from others, the combination can still be designated as a hedging instrument.

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.5].

<a id="s299-Para_46.132"></a>**46.132** IFRS 9 also permits derivative instruments to be combined with non-derivative instruments, or proportions of them, and the combination designated as the hedging instrument.

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.5].

<a id="s299-chap_46_navPoint_32"></a>
### ‘All-in-one’ hedges

 

<a id="s299-Para_46.133"></a>**46.133** An ‘all-in-one’ hedge is the designation of a gross settled derivative (that is, an instrument which is settled gross by delivery of the underlying asset and the payment of the price specified in the contract, rather than by net settlement of the difference between the two legs) as the hedging instrument in a cash flow hedge of the variability of the consideration to be paid or received in the future transaction that will occur on gross settlement of the derivative contract itself, assuming the other cash flow hedge accounting criteria are met.

<a id="s299-chap_46_navPoint_33"></a>
### Non-derivative financial instruments

 

<a id="s299-Para_46.134"></a>**46.134** Non-derivative financial instruments (or components of those instruments) can be used as hedging instruments subject to some limits.

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.2].

<a id="s299-Para_46.135"></a>**46.135** Hedging instruments must be designated in their entirety or as a proportion of the entire instrument (with limited exceptions, as described in [para 46.106](74.md#s299-Para_46.106) onwards). See also[ paragraph 46.141](74.md#s299-Para_46.141).

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.4]. [IFRS 9 App B para B6.2.5].

<a id="s299-Para_46.136"></a>**46.136** Only non-derivative financial instruments measured at FVTPL can normally be used as hedging instruments, with the exception described in [paragraph 46.139](74.md#s299-Para_46.139). Financial assets that do not meet the ‘solely payments of principal and interest’ requirements are carried at FVTPL. Financial assets measured in this way are eligible as hedging instruments.

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.2].

[FAQ 46.136.1 – Is it possible to use a non-derivative contract measured at FVTPL as a hedging instrument?](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.136.1)

<a id="s299-Para_46.137"></a>**46.137**Financial liabilities accounted for at FVTPL, but for which the changes in the liability’s own credit risk are presented in OCI, are not eligible for designation as hedging instruments. This is because such a designation would allow credit risk on the hedging instrument to be excluded from the hedge relationship or require the credit risk amount in OCI to be reclassified to P&amp;L.

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.2].

<a id="s299-Para_46.138"></a>**46.138**Instruments that were designated at inception at FVTPL to mitigate an accounting mismatch (commonly referred as the ‘fair value option’) might be later designated as hedging instruments. However, this should be consistent with an entity’s risk management objective and strategy, and hence, in practice, it should arise only if the original mismatch no longer exists.

[IFRS 9 para BC6.136]. [IFRS 9 para BC6.137]. [IFRS 9 para BC6.138]. [IFRS 9 para BC6.139].

<a id="s299-Para_46.139"></a>**46.139** The only exception to designating the hedging instrument in its entirety is that non-derivative financial instruments can be designated as hedging instruments of foreign currency risk, provided that such non-derivative financial instruments are not investments in equity instruments for which the entity has elected to present the changes in fair value in OCI. This is because the foreign currency risk component already needs to be separated and reported in P&amp;L in accordance with IAS 21.

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.2]. [IFRS 9 App B para B6.2.3]. [IFRS 9 para BC6.124].

[FAQ 46.139.1 – Is it possible to use a non-derivative financial instrument accounted for at amortised cost as a hedging instrument?](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.139.1)  
[FAQ 46.139.2 – Can an entity hedge forecasted foreign currency sales with IFRS 16 lease liabilities?](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.139.2)

<a id="s299-Para_46.140"></a>**46.140** Foreign currency debt investments (including cash deposits), and issued debt instruments carried at amortised cost or at FVOCI could be designated as a hedging instrument in a hedge of foreign currency risk. Similarly, a contract to purchase the non-controlling interest in a subsidiary which is required to be recognised as a liability under paragraph 23 of IAS 32 is not accounted for as a derivative and, accordingly, could be designated as a hedged item or a hedging instrument in a hedge of foreign currency risk only. A non-derivative item carried at amortised cost and used as a hedging instrument is fair valued for the hedged risk (foreign currency) only and not in totality, unlike derivative instruments or items carried at FVOCI.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.2.3].

[EX 46.140.1 – Examples of hedging with non-derivatives](77.md#s305-EX46.140.1)  
[FAQ 46.140.2 – How should an entity account for a cash flow hedge of a forecast sale where the hedging instrument is a term loan or issued bond?](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.140.2)

<a id="s299-Para_46.141"></a>**46.141** A proportion of a non-derivative financial instrument, such as 50% of the carrying amount of a foreign currency liability, could be designated as the hedging instrument. However, partial term designation is not allowed, that is, an entity cannot treat the cash flows of only a proportion of the period during which a non-derivative instrument designated as a hedge against foreign currency risk remains outstanding and exclude the other cash flows from the designated hedging relationship. For example, the cash flows during the first three years of a 10-year borrowing denominated in a foreign currency cannot qualify as a hedging instrument in a cash flow hedge of the first three years of revenue in the same foreign currency.

[IFRS 9 para 6.2.4(c)].

 

<a id="s299-chap_46_navPoint_34"></a>
### Items that do not qualify as hedging instrument

 

<a id="s299-Para_46.142"></a>**46.142** An entity’s own equity instruments are not financial assets or liabilities of the entity and, therefore, cannot be designated as hedging instruments. Similarly, non-controlling interests in consolidated financial statements are treated as part of equity and, hence, cannot be designated as hedging instruments in the consolidated financial statements.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.2.2].

[FAQ 46.142.1 – Is it possible to use a derivative on an entity's own equity instruments in a hedge relationship?](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.142.1)

<a id="s299-Para_46.143"></a>**46.143**Firm commitments and forecast transactions cannot be designated as hedging instruments, since they are not normally recognised in the financial statements. However, if the foreign currency component of a purchase commitment is required to be separated as an embedded derivative under paragraphs 4.3.3 and B4.3.8(d) of IFRS 9, it could be designated as a hedging instrument in a hedge of the exposure to changes in the fair value of the maturity amount of a debt attributable to foreign currency risk.

<a id="s299-Para_46.144"></a>**46.144** An acquirer must re-assess “designation of a derivative instrument as a hedging instrument in accordance with IFRS 9 … on the basis of the pertinent conditions as they exist at the acquisition date”. The acquirer is required to re-designate all hedge relationships of the acquired entity as if they started at the date of acquisition. As market rates are likely to have changed from the inception of the acquiree’s hedges, the underlying hypothetical derivative would have to be ‘reset’ to the market rates current at the time of the business combination and, hence, their fair value would then also be ‘reset’ to zero. The derivative itself will still be recorded at full fair value going forward, and its ‘off-market’ nature will be a source of ineffectiveness. See [paragraph 46.105](74.md#s299-Para_46.105) for further details.

[IFRS 3 para 16].

## Discontinuance of hedge accounting (paras 46.145 - 46. 154)

 

For hedges impacted by IBOR reform, Interest Rate Benchmark Reform Amendments to IFRS 9, IAS 39, IFRS 7, IFRS 4 and IFRS 16 are intended to ensure that an entity will not have to discontinue hedge accounting, solely because IBOR reform. It is likely that there will be changes to either or both the hedging instrument and hedged items.

Entities with financial asset and financial liability modifications in the context of IBOR reform should read the guidance in [chapter 42](63.md#s263-chap_42) and consider Phase 2 Interest Rate Benchmark Reform Amendments to IFRS 9, IAS 39, IFRS 7, IFRS 4 and IFRS 16. Refer to [Appendix 3 para A3.39](88.md#s379-Para_A3.39) for the Phase 2 amendments issued in August 2020.

  

<a id="s300-Para_46.145"></a>**46.145**Voluntary de-designation of hedge accounting is prohibited. Hedge accounting should be discontinued prospectively only when the hedging relationship (or part of the relationship) ceases to meet the qualifying criteria for hedge accounting. Examples of ceasing to meet the qualifying criteria include:

- The hedging instrument expires or is sold, terminated or exercised. For this purpose, the replacement or roll-over of a hedging instrument into another hedging instrument is not an expiration or termination if such replacement or roll-over is part of the entity’s documented risk management objective.

- The hedged item is sold, extinguished or no longer expected to occur.

- The hedging relationship no longer meets the risk management objective (for example, because the entity no longer pursues that risk management objective).

- The hedge effectiveness requirements are not met (for example, there is no longer an economic relationship between the hedged item and the hedging instrument, or the effect of credit risk starts to dominate the value changes that result from that economic relationship).

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.26]. [IFRS 9 para 6.5.6].

The accounting treatment when the hedging instrument or hedged item no longer exists is explained for fair value hedges and cash flow hedges in [paragraph 46.152](74.md#s300-Para_46.152) onwards and [46.153](74.md#s300-Para_46.153) onwards respectively.

[FAQ 46.145.1 – When could a change in risk management objective result in discontinuation of hedge accounting?](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.145.1)  
[FAQ 46.145.2 – Is it possible to continue to apply fair value hedge accounting following an intercompany transfer of the hedged item?](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.145.2)  
[FAQ 46.145.3 – Does an entity need to reassess the effectiveness of a net investment hedge if the carrying amount of the net investment changes?](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.145.3)

 

<a id="s300-chap_46_navPoint_36"></a>
### Risk management objective no longer met

 

<a id="s300-Para_46.146"></a>**46.146** If a hedging relationship no longer meets the risk management objective ([see paras 46.34 and 46.35](74.md#s297-Para_46.34)), hedge accounting must be discontinued. An entity should not de-designate and discontinue a hedging relationship if the hedging relationship still meets the original risk management objective that formed the basis for the hedge, assuming all other hedging criteria are still met.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.23].

[FAQ 46.146.1 – Is it possible to de-designate a hedge of foreign currency exposure on forecast purchases or sales under IFRS 9?](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.146.1)

<a id="s300-Para_46.147"></a>**46.147** A risk management strategy can (and often will) involve many different hedging relationships, each with a risk management objective. Hence, the risk management objective for a particular hedging relationship can change, even though an entity’s risk management strategy remains unchanged. If the risk management objective for a hedge relationship has changed, hedge accounting for the hedge relationship must be discontinued.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.24]. [IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.26].

<a id="s300-chap_46_navPoint_37"></a>
### Replacement or roll-over of hedging instruments

<a id="s300-Para_46.148"></a>**46.148** The replacement or roll-over of a hedging instrument into another hedging instrument is not an expiration or termination if such replacement or roll-over is part of the entity’s documented risk management objective. Any such replacement or roll-over should be documented at the level of the specific hedge relationship, as it refers to the documented risk management objective and not the higher-level hedging strategy.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.6].

<a id="s300-Para_46.149"></a>**46.149** A novation is not an expiration or termination of the hedging instrument if the novation is a consequence of laws or regulations with the purpose of incorporating a central clearing counterparty.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.6].

[FAQ 46.149.1 – What is the accounting for the novation of a derivative which was designated as a hedging instrument in a hedge relationship?](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.149.1)

<a id="s300-chap_46_navPoint_38"></a>
### Discontinuance of part of a hedging relationship

<a id="s300-Para_46.150"></a>**46.150** Discontinuance of hedge accounting can affect a hedging relationship in its entirety or only part of it, depending on the facts and circumstances. Examples of where only a part of a hedging relationship is discontinued are:

- Where the volume of hedged item included in the hedging relationship has decreased due to an adjustment to the hedge ratio as part of a rebalancing exercise (see [para 46.50](74.md#s297-Para_46.50) onwards).

- Where part of the volume of a forecast transaction is no longer highly probable.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.25]. [IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.27].

<a id="s300-Para_46.151"></a>**46.151** Hedge accounting continues for the remainder of the hedging relationship in these circumstances. However, for hedges of forecast transactions, if an entity constantly revises its forecasts, this might call into question whether future forecast volumes or transactions are highly probable, and therefore eligible as hedged items.

[IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.25]. [IFRS 9 App B para B6.5.27].

## Alternatives to hedge accounting (paras 46.155- paras 46.161)

<a id="s301-chap_46_navPoint_43"></a>
### Extended use of fair value option for ‘own use’ contracts

 

<a id="s301-Para_46.155"></a>**46.155** An entity can irrevocably designate and measure certain items at FVTPL in certain circumstances (‘fair value’ option) – see [chapter 42 para 32](63.md#s265-Para_42.32) for financial assets and [chapter 42 para 74](63.md#s266-Para_42.74) for financial liabilities. Entities are allowed to apply the fair value option where either doing so eliminates or significantly reduces an accounting mismatch, or a group of financial liabilities or liabilities and assets are managed on a fair value basis.

[IFRS 9 para 4.1.5].

<a id="s301-Para_46.156"></a>**46.156** Entities are able to apply this fair value option irrevocably to contracts to buy or sell non-financial items which qualify for the ‘own use’ exception on initial recognition if it eliminates or reduces an accounting mismatch. These are contracts that can be settled net or by exchanging financial instruments, or where the non-financial item is readily convertible to cash but that were entered into for the purpose of the receipt or delivery of a non-financial item in accordance with the entity’s expected purchase, sale or usage requirements. This will eliminate a common mismatch for non-financial entities, such as entities in the utilities industry. Note that this choice can be made on a contract-by-contract basis.

[IFRS 9 para 2.5].

<a id="s301-chap_46_navPoint_idp1687459288"></a>
#### Option to designate a credit exposure at FVTPL

 

<a id="s301-Para_46.157"></a>**46.157**The credit risk of a financial instrument, such as a loan or loan commitment, is a risk component that does not meet the eligibility criteria to be designated as a hedged item. The spread between the risk-free rate and the market interest rate incorporates credit risk, liquidity risk, funding risk and other unidentified risk components and margin elements. Credit risk cannot be isolated, and so does not meet the ‘separately identifiable’ criterion.

[IFRS 9 para BC6.470]. [IFRS 9 para BC6.503]. [IFRS 9 para BC6.504].

<a id="s301-Para_46.158"></a>**46.158** Credit derivatives, such as credit default swaps that are used to hedge credit risk, are accounted for at FVTPL, while credit exposures are usually measured at amortised cost or are unrecognised (such as, loan commitments). Where there is credit deterioration, gains are recognised on the credit derivative, while the impairment on the hedged item is measured on a different basis, which results in P&amp;L volatility that does not reflect the credit protection obtained.

<a id="s301-Para_46.159"></a>**46.159**There is an option to designate the financial instrument (or a proportion of it) at FVTPL This option is available only where credit derivatives are used to manage all (or a proportion) of the credit risk on the instrument and if:

- the name of the credit exposure (for example, the borrower or the holder of a loan commitment) matches the reference entity of the credit derivative (‘name matching’); and

- the seniority of the financial instrument matches that of the instruments that can be delivered in accordance with the credit derivative.

[IFRS 9 para 6.7.1].

[FAQ 46.159.1 – Are index-based credit default swaps eligible hedging instruments for a portfolio of financial assets?](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.159.1)  
[FAQ 46.159.2 – If the fair value option is elected, would there be a perfect offset between the credit exposure and the credit derivative?](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.159.2)  
[FAQ 46.159.3 – When can an entity designate a financial instrument at fair value through profit or loss in order to help it manage credit risk?](77.md#s305-FAQ_46.159.3)

<a id="s301-Para_46.160"></a>**46.160** This fair value option is different from the irrevocable option for ‘own use’ contracts (as described in [paras 46.155](74.md#s301-Para_46.155) and [46.156](74.md#s301-Para_46.156)), since this can be elected at initial recognition, subsequently or even while the hedged credit exposure is unrecognised (for example, in the case of a loan commitment). However, if a financial instrument is designated at FVTPL after its initial recognition, or was previously not recognised, the difference at the time of designation between the carrying amount, if any, and the fair value is recognised immediately in P&amp;L.

[IFRS 9 para 6.7.1]. [IFRS 9 para 6.7.2].

<a id="s301-Para_46.161"></a>**46.161** In addition, this fair value option is not irrevocable but, once this option is elected, the entity should only discontinue fair valuing the financial instrument giving rise to the credit risk if the qualifying criteria above are no longer met and there is no other reason (for example, a change in the business model) why the item should continue to be carried at FVTPL.

[IFRS 9 para 6.7.3].

## Disclosures and effective date (paras 46.162-46.173)

 

<a id="s302-Para_46.162"></a>**46.162** The disclosure requirements for hedge accounting are extensive.

<a id="s302-Para_46.163"></a>**46.163** The disclosures are required for each category of risk that an entity decides to hedge (for example, interest rate risk, foreign currency risk or commodity price risk). An entity should apply judgement and categorise risks on the basis of how it manages its risks through hedging. However, an entity should apply its risk categories consistently throughout the entire hedge accounting disclosures.

<a id="s302-Para_46.164"></a>**46.164** The required hedge accounting disclosures in IFRS 7 are very detailed and provide information about:

- an entity’s risk management strategy and how it is applied to manage risk;

- how the entity’s hedging activities might affect the amount, timing and uncertainty of its future cash flows;

- the effect that hedge accounting has had on the entity’s financial statements; and

- whether the entity is applying the option to designate a credit exposure as measured at FVTPL.

See the PwC Illustrative accounts and [chapter 47](78.md#s306-chap_47) for further details and examples.

[IFRS 7 para 21A]. [IFRS 7 para 21B]. [IFRS 7 para 21C]. [IFRS 7 para 21D]. [IFRS 7 para 24G].

### Effective date and transition

 

<a id="s302-Para_46.165"></a>**46.165**IFRS 9 was effective for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2018. Earlier application was permitted. In September 2019, the IASB issued amendments to IFRS 9 (and IAS 39) to support the provision of useful information during the period of uncertainty arising from the phasing out of interest-rate benchmarks such as IBORs ([see para 46.4.1](74.md#s296-Para_46.4.1)). Early adoption was permitted in 2019 accounts. In August 2020, the IASB issued further amendments to support the continuation of hedge accounting despite changes made as a result of IBOR reform. The amendment is effective for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2021. Early adoption is permitted.

[IFRS 9 para 7.1.1].

<a id="s302-Para_46.166"></a>**46.166** IFRS 9 was applied retrospectively (except for aspects of hedge accounting), but comparatives are not required to be restated (with some minor exceptions for hedge accounting – [see para 46.171](74.md#s302-Para_46.171)).

[IFRS 9 para 7.2.1].

<a id="s302-Para_46.167"></a>**46.167** However, for annual periods beginning before 1 January 2018, an entity was able to apply only the own credit risk presentation requirements for financial liabilities. Entities can early adopt the requirement to recognise in OCI the changes in fair value attributable to changes in an entity’s own credit risk in financial liabilities that are designated under the fair value option.

[IFRS 9 para 7.1.2].

<a id="s302-Para_46.168"></a>**46.168** Entities have an accounting policy choice to apply IFRS 9 hedge accounting or to continue applying IAS 39 hedge accounting. This accounting policy is applied to all of the entity’s hedge relationships. To apply hedge accounting from the date of adoption of IFRS 9, all qualifying criteria must be met as at that date.

[IFRS 9 para 7.2.21]. [IFRS 9 para 7.2.23].

<a id="s302-Para_46.169"></a>**46.169**Hedging relationships that qualified for hedge accounting in accordance with IAS 39, that also qualify for hedge accounting in accordance with IFRS 9 (after taking into account any rebalancing of the hedging relationship on transition), are regarded as continuing hedging relationships. Any gain or loss from such rebalancing must be recognised in P&amp;L.

[IFRS 9 para 7.2.24].
