## Introduction and scope (paras 51.1-51.4)

<a id="s349-chap_51_navPoint_2"></a>
### Introduction and scope

 

<a id="s349-Para_51.1"></a>**51.1** This chapter covers the general accounting and disclosure requirements for share capital and reserves and treasury shares. There is little guidance in IFRS in some areas. Instead, local legal requirements might dictate the accounting and disclosure requirements in some territories.

<a id="s349-Para_51.2"></a>**51.2** Assets, liabilities and equity are the three elements used to measure financial position. ‘Equity’ is the residual interest in the entity’s assets after deducting its liabilities, equal to its net assets.

[IAS 32 para 11].

<a id="s349-Para_51.3"></a>**51.3** There are no requirements in IAS 32 on the initial recognition and measurement of equity. In many instances, equity is recorded as the proceeds of issue, net of transaction costs, and is not subsequently remeasured.

 

<a id="s349-chap_51_navPoint_3"></a>
### Nature and characteristics of equity

<a id="s349-chap_51_navPoint_4"></a>
### Types of share capital and reserves

 

<a id="s349-Para_51.4"></a>**51.4** IAS 32 provides guidance on the classification of shares as equity or debt. The accounting conclusion might be different from the legal form of the shares, because the legal form might be determined based on local legislation. Guidance on the classification and accounting for shares as either debt or equity under IAS 32 and IFRIC 2 is discussed further in [chapter 43 para 3](67.md#s275-Para_43.3).

[IAS 32 para 2].

[FAQ 51.4.2 – What are the typical types of reserves?](85.md#s354-FAQ_51.4.2)

## Equity arising from transactions with shareholders (paras 51.5 - 51.39)

<a id="s350-chap_51_navPoint_6"></a>
### Share capital accounted for as equity

 

<a id="s350-Para_51.5"></a>**51.5** IAS 32 does not provide guidance on the initial recognition or measurement of equity. The increase in equity on the issue of equity shares is normally recorded as the residual after recording the recognition or de-recognition of assets or liabilities arising on the share issue (the proceeds of issue) and after deducting directly attributable transaction costs.

[IAS 32 para 2].

<a id="s350-Para_51.6"></a>**51.6** Transaction costs of an equity instrument are taken to equity.

[IAS 32 para 35].

<a id="s350-Para_51.7"></a>**51.7** The tax associated with the transaction costs, or with any distributions on the shares, is accounted for in accordance with IAS 12.

[IAS 32 para 35A].

[FAQ 51.7.1 – What other requirements are there in respect of share capital?](85.md#s354-FAQ_51.7.1)

 

<a id="s350-chap_51_navPoint_8"></a>
### Share capital accounted for as a liability

<a id="s350-Para_51.8"></a>**51.8**Some share issues might not qualify as equity and are presented as financial liabilities. This is discussed further in [chapter 43 para 12](67.md#s276-Para_43.12). Local legal requirements might require disclosure of the legal capital and any premium on shares classified as a financial liability.

[IAS 32 para 16].

<a id="s350-chap_51_navPoint_9"></a>
### Compound instruments

<a id="s350-Para_51.9"></a>**51.9** Instruments consisting of equity and liability components, such as convertible debt, are split into these components, and each is accounted for separately. This is discussed further in [chapter 43 para 51](67.md#s278-Para_43.51).

[IAS 32 para 28].

<a id="s350-chap_51_navPoint_10"></a>
### Partly paid equity shares

<a id="s350-Para_51.10"></a>**51.10** Shares could be issued fully or partly paid. IFRS contains no guidance on how such shares should be accounted for.

[IAS 32 para 2].

[FAQ 51.10.1 – How should partly paid shares be accounted for?](85.md#s354-FAQ_51.10.1)

<a id="s350-chap_51_navPoint_12"></a>
### Shares to be issued

<a id="s350-Para_51.11"></a>**51.11** Companies might receive consideration for shares before the shares are issued. IFRS contains no guidance on how such transactions should be accounted for.

[IAS 32 para 2].

[FAQ 51.11.1 – How should shares to be issued be accounted for?](85.md#s354-FAQ_51.11.1)

<a id="s350-chap_51_navPoint_13"></a>
### Allotment of shares for non-cash consideration

<a id="s350-Para_51.12"></a>**51.12** Companies might allot shares, including any premium, for money or for other items that can be valued in money, such as fixed assets or intellectual property. IFRS contains no guidance on how such transactions should be accounted for.

[IAS 32 para 2].

[FAQ 51.12.1 – How should shares issued for non-cash consideration be accounted for?](85.md#s354-FAQ_51.12.1)

<a id="s350-chap_51_navPoint_15"></a>
### Multi-currency share capital

 

<a id="s350-Para_51.13"></a>**51.13** Companies might issue share capital denominated in a currency other than their functional currency. The accounting implications of this are covered in [chapter 49](80.md#s318-chap_49) [EX 49.46.2](80.md#s324-EX49.46.2).

[IAS 21 para 23(c)].

 

<a id="s350-chap_51_navPoint_17"></a>
### Issue costs

 

<a id="s350-Para_51.14"></a>**51.14** Under IAS 1, “equity capital and reserves are disaggregated into various classes, such as paid-in capital, share premium and reserves”, either on the face of the balance sheet or in the notes. In some jurisdictions, legal capital maintenance requirements govern the creation and use of the share premium account.

[IAS 1 para 78(e)].

<a id="s350-Para_51.15"></a>**51.15** Only the costs of a successful issue can be written off directly in equity. See [chapter 43 para 89](67.md#s280-Para_43.89) for further guidance on the treatment of transaction costs of equity instruments.

[IAS 32 para 35].

<a id="s350-Para_51.16"></a>**51.16** Warrants are typically an equity instrument under IAS 32, so any costs of issue are recorded in equity.

[IAS 32 para 22].

 

<a id="s350-chap_51_navPoint_19"></a>
### Capital redemption reserve

 

<a id="s350-Para_51.17"></a>**51.17** National laws in some jurisdictions require reserves to be created for capital maintenance purposes, but the accounting is not covered by IFRS.

[IAS 32 para 2].

[FAQ 51.17.1 – Why might a capital redemption reserve need to be created?](85.md#s354-FAQ_51.17.1)

 

<a id="s350-chap_51_navPoint_21"></a>
### Capital contributions

<a id="s350-Para_51.18"></a>**51.18** Contributions are non-reciprocal in nature (that is, by way of a gift). There is no guidance in IFRS for the accounting for capital contributions. When transacting with related parties on a non-arm’s-length transaction or at a price that is not reflective of fair value, an entity should consider whether there has been a capital contribution or a distribution.

[IAS 32 para 2].

[FAQ 51.18.1 – How is a capital contribution accounted for?](85.md#s354-FAQ_51.18.1)

<a id="s350-chap_51_navPoint_22"></a>
### Distributions/dividends

 

<a id="s350-Para_51.19"></a>**51.19** Distributions include dividends on equity shares and coupons on other instruments that are classified as equity. Distributions to holders of equity instruments are debited directly to equity.

[IAS 32 para 35].

<a id="s350-Para_51.20"></a>**51.20** The corresponding entry is made to cash if paid immediately, or recognised as a liability where settlement is to be made at a future date. An issuer of non-puttable ordinary shares recognises a liability when it formally acts to make a distribution and becomes legally obligated to the shareholders to do so.

[IAS 32 para AG13].

<a id="s350-Para_51.21"></a>**51.21** Where there is no obligation at the balance sheet date, a liability is not recorded. A history of paying dividends does not give rise to a contractual obligation or a constructive obligation. See [chapter 9 para 16](17.md#s56-Para_9.16) for further details.

[IAS 37 para 14].

<a id="s350-Para_51.22"></a>**51.22** Dividends payable on shares accounted for as financial liabilities are recognised as an expense in the income statement, and subsequently as a repayment of the liability when settled. Compound financial instruments that are divided into their liability and equity components have a consequential effect on the amounts recognised as interest expense and distributions. See [chapter 43 para 87](67.md#s280-Para_43.87) for further details.

[IAS 32 para 35].

[FAQ 51.22.1 – What form can a distribution take?](85.md#s354-FAQ_51.22.1)  
[FAQ 51.22.2 – When should a dividend be recognised?](85.md#s354-FAQ_51.22.2)  
[FAQ 51.22.3 – How should scrip dividends be accounted for?](85.md#s354-FAQ_51.22.3)

 

<a id="s350-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689304232"></a>
#### Unlawful dividends

 

<a id="s350-Para_51.23"></a>**51.23**The legality of dividends is determined by national law and not by IFRS. If the recipient is contractually required to repay a dividend that is found to be unlawful, for example because there is explicit reference in the terms of the share instruments in respect of which the dividends have been paid, the payer recognises a financial asset, recorded initially at fair value. The original transaction is not reversed. Absent the existence of anything contractual obligating the recipient to repay the dividend, the payer recognises a contingent asset in the period when it is virtually certain that payment will be received. Prior to the receipt becoming virtually certain, the payer discloses the existence of the contingent asset where it is probable that payment will be received.

[IFRS 9 para 5.1 (IAS 39 para 43)]. [IAS 37 para 31]. [IAS 37 para 32]. [IAS 37 para 33]. [IAS 37 para 34]. [IAS 37 para 35].

 

<a id="s350-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689321768"></a>
#### Accounting for distributions in kind

 

<a id="s350-Para_51.24"></a>**51.24** An entity might distribute non-cash assets. The entity debits distributions to equity.

[IAS 32 para 35].

<a id="s350-Para_51.25"></a>**51.25** IAS 32 does not specify the amount to be debited, and whether the assets should be accounted for at book value or fair value of the assets transferred. IFRIC 17 specifically provides guidance on these matters. For further guidance, see [chapter 30 para 58](53.md#s219-Para_30.58).

[IFRIC 17 para 3].

 

<a id="s350-chap_51_navPoint_23"></a>
### Treasury shares

<a id="s350-Para_51.26"></a>**51.26** Treasury shares are defined as re-acquired own equity instruments. This definition is wider than equity shares and includes share options. National law might determine whether entities can re-acquire their shares and hold them in treasury. Management should also consider the consequences of any statutory and common law rules on capital maintenance and distributions.

[IAS 32 para 33].

<a id="s350-chap_51_navPoint_25"></a>
### Accounting treatment for the purchase of own shares (including treasury shares)

 

<a id="s350-Para_51.27"></a>**51.27** If an entity re-acquires its own equity instruments, those instruments should be deducted from equity. Subject to the paragraph below, the accounting is as follows:

- No gain or loss should be recognised in profit or loss on the purchase, sale, issue or cancellation of an entity’s own equity instruments.

- Consideration paid or received for the purchase or sale of an entity’s own equity instruments should be recognised directly in equity.

[IAS 32 para 33].

<a id="s350-Para_51.28"></a>**51.28** The amount paid for the repurchased instruments is deducted from equity. Where equity shares have been purchased for cancellation, national law and/or the entity’s constitution might dictate whether the deduction is made against paid-in capital or distributable profits. The accounting, therefore, should reflect the relevant capital maintenance requirements of national law. Similarly, for equity shares purchased for holding in treasury, national law might require this amount to be deducted from distributable profits. In the absence of any legal requirement, the amount could either be debited to a separate component of equity or be set against retained earnings. Paid-in capital is not reduced.

[IAS 32 para 33].

<a id="s350-Para_51.29"></a>**51.29** Often, entities place contracts with brokers or banks to acquire own shares on their behalf. Contracts containing an obligation for an entity to purchase its own equity instruments for cash or another financial asset are required to give rise to a financial liability. The financial liability should be measured initially at the present value of the redemption amount (for example, for the present value of the forward repurchase price, option exercise price or other redemption amount). When the liability is recognised, the corresponding debit is to equity.

[IAS 32 para 23].

<a id="s350-Para_51.30"></a>**51.30** Subsequently, the financial liability should be measured in accordance with IFRS 9 (IAS 39), and this might well result in gains or losses being recognised in the income statement.

[IFRS 9 App B para B5.4.6 (IAS 39 para AG 8)].

<a id="s350-Para_51.31"></a>**51.31** The entity accounts for the acquisition of the ‘treasury shares’ at the inception of the contract, and not when the shares are paid for.

[IFRS 9 para 3.1.1 (IAS 39 para 14)].

<a id="s350-Para_51.32"></a>**51.32** If the contract expires without delivery or full delivery, the amount of the financial liability attributable to the undelivered shares is reclassified to equity, reversing the original entry.

[IAS 32 para 23].

<a id="s350-Para_51.33"></a>**51.33**An entity has a contractual obligation to purchase its own equity instruments, even if the obligation to purchase, re-acquire or redeem is conditional on the counterparty exercising its rights (for example, a written put option giving the counterparty the right to sell an entity’s own equity instruments to the entity for a fixed price). The entity recognises a financial liability for the present value of the purchase, re-acquisition or redemption amount.

[IAS 32 para 23].

[FAQ 51.33.1 – How should the liability for acquisition of own shares be measured?](85.md#s354-FAQ_51.33.1)

<a id="s350-Para_51.34"></a>**51.34** Expenses directly relating to the purchase of treasury shares are treated as part of the overall cost of purchase and, like the purchase cost, are deducted from equity. See [chapter 43 para 79](67.md#s280-Para_43.79) for the treatment of transaction costs of equity instruments.

[IAS 32 para 37].

<a id="s350-Para_51.35"></a>**51.35** Own equity instruments might be acquired and held in treasury by the entity or by other members of the consolidated group. In consolidated financial statements, subsidiaries’ interests in the parent’s equity instruments are deducted from equity. This applies to all such shareholdings, including those held by subsidiaries that carry on a business of dealing in securities.

[IAS 32 para 33].

<a id="s350-Para_51.36"></a>**51.36** The guidance above on treasury shares also applies to treasury shares sold, issued or cancelled in connection with employee share option plans, employee share purchase plans and all other share-based payment arrangements. These plans are addressed in [chapter 13](24.md#s85-chap_13).

[IAS 32 para 4(f)(ii)].

<a id="s350-Para_51.37"></a>**51.37** Where shares classified as financial liabilities are re-acquired, the financial liability is removed, provided the de-recognition criteria are met (see [chapter 44 para 105](68.md#s286-Para_44.105)). There might be local legal capital maintenance requirements applicable to the re-purchase of the shares and consequential accounting impacts.

[IFRS 9 para 3.3.1 (IAS 39 para 39)].

[FAQ 51.37.1 – How should shares held in group undertakings be treated in individual financial statements?](85.md#s354-FAQ_51.37.1)

 

<a id="s350-chap_51_navPoint_27"></a>
### Non-controlling interests

 

<a id="s350-Para_51.38"></a>**51.38** Non-controlling interests in consolidated financial statements are presented as a component of equity, separate from the equity of the owners of the parent. Non-controlling interest is the equity in a subsidiary that is not attributable, directly or indirectly, to a parent. Chapter 26 para 183 addresses the accounting for non-controlling interests.

[IFRS 3 App A]. [IAS 1 para 54(q)].

 

<a id="s350-chap_51_navPoint_28"></a>
### Entities without shares

<a id="s350-Para_51.39"></a>**51.39**Entities such as partnerships and trusts do not have share capital. The accounting treatment for the interests that they issue will be governed by the requirements of IAS 32/IFRS 9 (IAS 39). Separate legal considerations might apply to partnership and trust interests in different jurisdictions.

[IAS 32 para 4].

## Reserves arising from the presentation of gains and losses (paras 51.40 - 51.45)

 

<a id="s351-Para_51.40"></a>**51.40** Reserves, together with equity share capital and other own equity instruments, make up the shareholders’ equity section of an entity’s balance sheet. The IASB Framework states that equity is the residual interest in the assets of the entity, after deducting all of its liabilities. Equity claims are claims on the residual interest in the assets of the entity, after deducting all of its liabilities. Such claims can be established by contract, legislation or similar means, and they include (to the extent that they do not meet the definition of a liability) shares of various types, issued by the entity, and some obligations of the entity to issue another equity claim. Different classes of equity claim can confer different rights on their holders (for example, rights to receive dividends or rights on liquidation). Sometimes, legal, regulatory or other requirements affect particular components of equity, such as share capital or retained earnings. The IASB Framework states that, to provide useful information, it might be necessary to classify components of equity separately if some of those components are subject to particular legal, regulatory or other requirements. For example, in some jurisdictions, an entity is permitted to make distributions to holders of equity claims only if the entity has sufficient reserves specified as distributable. Separate classification of those reserves might provide useful information, particularly where they indicate legal or other restrictions on the ability of the entity to distribute or otherwise apply its equity. They might also reflect the fact that parties with ownership interests in an entity have differing rights in relation to the receipt of dividends or the repayment of contributed equity. Reserves are frequently referred to as components of equity.

[Framework paras 4.63–4.67]. [Framework para 7.12]. [Framework para 7.13].

 

<a id="s351-chap_51_navPoint_30"></a>
### Revaluation reserve

 

<a id="s351-Para_51.41"></a>**51.41** Under IFRS 16, IAS 16 and IAS 38, entities can account for certain assets at a revalued amount. Revaluation surpluses are recognised in other comprehensive income and accumulated in equity under the heading of ‘revaluation surplus’ or ‘revaluation reserve’ (see chapter 15 para 87 for IFRS 16, chapter 21 para 91 for IAS 38 and chapter 22 para 76 for IAS 16).

[IFRS 16 para 35]. [IAS 16 para 39]. [IAS 38 para 85].

 

<a id="s351-chap_51_navPoint_31"></a>
### Fair value reserve

 

<a id="s351-Para_51.42"></a>**51.42** IFRS requires gains and losses to be taken directly to other comprehensive income on the re-measurement of certain financial assets (debt instruments held within a ‘collect and sell’ business model where contractual cash flows are solely payments of principal and interest and equity shares where an irrevocable election to present fair value changes in other comprehensive income has been made) (IAS 39 financial assets classified as available for sale) but does not specify any particular reserve to accumulate the gains and losses. However, national law might include such a specification. It has become the convention that they are credited to a ‘fair value’ reserve.

[IFRS 9 para 4.1.2A (IAS 39 para 9)]. [IFRS 9 para 5.7.5]. [IFRS 9 para 5.7.10 (IAS 39 para 55(b))].

 

<a id="s351-chap_51_navPoint_32"></a>
### Hedging reserve

 

<a id="s351-Para_51.43"></a>**51.43** The effective portion of gains and losses arising from the re-measurement of a financial instrument designated as the hedging instrument in a cash flow hedge is recognised in other comprehensive income. See [chapter 46 para 15](74.md#s296-Para_46.15) and chapter 6.8A para 128 for further details.

[IFRS 9 para 6.5.11 (IAS 39 para 95)].

 

<a id="s351-chap_51_navPoint_33"></a>
### Foreign currency translation reserve

 

<a id="s351-Para_51.44"></a>**51.44** Exchange differences that arise on translation of a foreign operation are recognised in other comprehensive income and accumulated as a separate component of equity. Gains or losses that arise on hedging instruments, that are effective hedges against the impact of changes in exchange rates on the net assets of a foreign operation (a ‘net investment hedge’), are included in the consolidated foreign currency translation reserve.

[IAS 21 para 32].

 

<a id="s351-chap_51_navPoint_34"></a>
### Retained earnings

 

<a id="s351-Para_51.45"></a>**51.45** IAS 1 does not define what is meant by retained earnings.

[IAS 1 para 7].

## Disclosure requirements (paras 51.46 - 51.58)

<a id="s352-chap_51_navPoint_36"></a>
### General requirements

<a id="s352-Para_51.46"></a>**51.46** At a minimum, IAS 1 requires the issued capital and reserves attributable to owners of the parent and non-controlling interests to be presented on the face of the balance sheet.

[IAS 1 para 54].

<a id="s352-Para_51.47"></a>**51.47** IAS 1 requires an entity to disclose sub-classifications of the line items presented in the balance sheet, either on the face of the balance sheet or in the notes. Equity capital and reserves are disaggregated into classes such as paid-in capital, share premium and reserves.

[IAS 1 para 77]. [IAS 1 para 78].

<a id="s352-chap_51_navPoint_37"></a>
### Share capital

 

<a id="s352-Para_51.48"></a>**51.48** IAS 1 has specific disclosure requirements for share capital. Disclosures about share capital in an entity’s financial statements might also be required by national law, or by a regulator or under the terms of the market on which the shares might be traded.

[IAS 1 para 79]. [IAS 1 para 80].

[FAQ 51.48.1 – Do the disclosure requirements only apply to share capital accounted for as equity?](85.md#s354-FAQ_51.48.1)

<a id="s352-chap_51_navPoint_38"></a>
### Treasury shares

 

<a id="s352-Para_51.49"></a>**51.49** The ‘amount’ of equity treasury shares held is disclosed separately, either on the face of the balance sheet or in the notes. We believe that the reference in IAS 32 to the ‘amount held’ is the number and class of shares held. Note that this disclosure requirement, in an entity’s separate financial statements, also encompasses the entity’s shares held by subsidiaries (including EBTs and ESOPs) and associates, even though these shares are not deducted from equity in the parent’s separate balance sheet. Similarly, in consolidated financial statements, the disclosure requirement in IAS 1 includes shares held by associates, even though they are not deducted from equity (and are not treasury shares), because associates are not part of the consolidated group.

[IAS 32 para 34]. [IAS 1 para 79(a)(vi)]. [IAS 1 para 79(vi)].

<a id="s352-Para_51.50"></a>**51.50** If the entity re-acquires its own equity instruments from related parties, IAS 24 applies.

[IAS 24 para 15].

<a id="s352-Para_51.51"></a>**51.51** Entities whose shares are publicly traded observe the rules of the market and/or the relevant regulator governing the purchase of shares for cancellation or to hold in treasury. Such rules might also dictate certain disclosures to be made in the entities’ consolidated financial statements.

[IAS 32 para 34].

 

<a id="s352-chap_51_navPoint_39"></a>
### Reserves

 

<a id="s352-Para_51.52"></a>**51.52** The statement of changes in equity requires a reconciliation between the carrying amount of each component of equity at the beginning and the end of the period, separately disclosing each change.

[IAS 1 para 106].

<a id="s352-Para_51.53"></a>**51.53** Distributions are normally made from profits available for distribution of individual companies, rather than from groups. Users of financial statements might nonetheless draw inferences from the amount shown on the consolidated retained profits reserve. Where there are significant restrictions (for example, resulting from borrowing arrangements or regulatory requirements) on the ability of subsidiaries to transfer funds to the parent in the form of cash dividends or to repay loans or advances, the nature and extent of the restrictions is disclosed.

[IFRS 12 para 13].

[FAQ 51.53.2 – What is required to be disclosed in respect of distributable reserves?](85.md#s354-FAQ_51.53.2)

### Statement of changes in equity

<a id="s352-Para_51.54"></a>**51.54** Under IAS 1, changes in equity are presented in a primary statement. Changes in equity attributable to owners of the parent are shown separately in the statement from changes attributable to non-controlling interests.

[IAS 1 para 106].

<a id="s352-Para_51.55"></a>**51.55** For each component of equity, an analysis of other comprehensive income by item can either be presented in the statement of changes in equity or be disclosed in the notes. The statement of changes in equity and the statement of comprehensive income are addressed in [chapter 4 para 138](10.md#s29-Para_4.138).

[IAS 1 para 109].

### Capital disclosures

 

<a id="s352-Para_51.56"></a>**51.56** An entity is required to disclose information that will enable users of its financial statements to evaluate the entity’s objectives, policies and processes for managing capital.

[IAS 1 para 134].

<a id="s352-Para_51.57"></a>**51.57** The detailed disclosure requirements should be based on the information provided internally to key management personnel.

[IAS 1 para 135].

<a id="s352-Para_51.58"></a>**51.58** An entity might manage capital in a number of ways and be subject to a number of different capital requirements. For example, a group might include both banking and insurance activities, or a non-financial services group might have a financial services subsidiary. In such circumstances, where aggregate capital disclosures would not provide useful information or would distort a user’s understanding of an entity’s capital resources, the entity should disclose separate information for each capital requirement to which it is subject.

[IAS 1 para 136].

### Nature and characteristics of equity

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689335080"></a>
#### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.4.1"></a>FAQ 51.4.1 – What are the different classes of share capital that could exist?

Reference to standard: IAS 32 para 2  
 Reference to standing text: [51.4](85.md#s349-Para_51.4) 

  

  
There are numerous types of shares, including ordinary shares, preference shares, non-voting shares and redeemable shares.

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689397672"></a>
#### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.4.2"></a>FAQ 51.4.2 – What are the typical types of reserves?

Reference to standard: IAS 32 para 2  
 Reference to standing text: [51.4](85.md#s349-Para_51.4) 

  

  
 IFRS does not define reserves. Some reserves result from accounting requirements (for example, fair value and foreign currency translation reserves), to reflect certain measurement changes in other comprehensive income rather than the income statement. These separate reserves are often reclassified (‘recycled’) from other comprehensive income to the income statement when the related assets and liabilities are de-recognised or impaired. Other reserves arise from jurisdictional requirements.

Typical reserves include:

- Share premium account.

- Capital redemption reserve (see [para 51.17](85.md#s350-Para_51.17)).

- Revaluation reserve (see [para 51.41](85.md#s351-Para_51.41)).

- Fair value reserve (see [para 51.42](85.md#s351-Para_51.42)).

- Hedging reserve (see [para 51.43](85.md#s351-Para_51.43)).

- Foreign currency translation reserve (see [para 51.44](85.md#s351-Para_51.44)).

- Capital reserve.

- Retained profits (see [para 51.45](85.md#s351-Para_51.45)).

- Other reserves, including reserves arising from local statutory requirements.

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_46"></a>
### Equity arising from transactions with shareholders – recognition and measurement

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689417384"></a>
#### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.7.1"></a>FAQ 51.7.1 – What other requirements are there in respect of share capital?

Reference to standard: IAS 32 para 2  
 Reference to standing text: [51.7](85.md#s350-Para_51.7) 

  

  
Local legal requirements might determine whether the shares have any nominal value and whether any premium needs to be credited to a legal reserve or other reserve. Local requirements will also determine whether these reserves are available for use (for example, to meet issue costs).

A company can issue shares for any amount and type of consideration that the laws of the country in which it is established permit. Changes in the fair value (market value) of an equity share do not affect the amount at which it is stated in the financial statements. It is not re-measured after initial recognition.

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689432616"></a>
#### Partly paid equity shares

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689429800"></a>
##### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.10.1"></a>FAQ 51.10.1 – How should partly paid shares be accounted for?

 

Reference to standard: IAS 32 para 2  
 Reference to standing text: [51.10](85.md#s350-Para_51.10) 

  

  
For fully paid equity shares, the amount recognised in equity is equal to the proceeds of issue received by the issuer. The accounting treatment for partly paid shares depends on the terms and conditions of the share issue and possibly the company’s constitution. The issuer of equity shares records at least the proceeds received (less directly attributable transaction costs) on the partly paid shares as a credit to equity. Whether the issuer will recognise the balance not yet paid on the shares as a receivable, with a corresponding credit to equity, will depend on whether the issuer has a present right to receive the consideration.

For example, a company issues partly paid shares. The share agreement sets out a fixed schedule of definite call dates when the shareholders must pay instalments of the outstanding amount on the shares. The shareholders cannot avoid the calls. The company’s constitution or national law determines whether the schedule of calls legally obliges the shareholders to make the scheduled payments.

If the shareholder is contractually obliged to pay, the issuer recognises a receivable for the outstanding future receipts (discounted, if material) at the issue date of the shares, with a corresponding increase in equity (share capital/share premium depending on local legal requirements).

The receivable is a financial asset, as the issuer has a contractual right to receive cash from the shareholder (and equally the shareholder has a financial liability to pay the issuer). It is measured at fair value on initial recognition and subsequently accounted for under IFRS 9, for more details [see chapter 42](63.md#s263-chap_42) (under IAS 39 see chapter 6.7A).

However, if shareholders can avoid calls on partly paid shares, this is not a financial asset and no equity is recognised until the shareholder has paid or has committed to the call making it irrevocable.

Accounting for share purchases by employees where the funding for the share purchase has been provided by the company is dealt with in [chapter 13](24.md#s85-chap_13).

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689450536"></a>
#### Shares to be issued

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689465256"></a>
##### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.11.1"></a>FAQ 51.11.1 – How should shares to be issued be accounted for?

 

Reference to standard: IAS 32 para 2  
 Reference to standing text: [51.11](85.md#s350-Para_51.11) 

  

  
Whether shares have been issued is a legal matter. If the company believes that a shareholder is legally entitled to the shares (for example, is entitled to dividends), the consideration received is credited to paid-in capital (share capital/share premium), regardless of whether a share certificate has been issued. If the shares have not been legally issued, the consideration received is credited to ‘shares to be issued’.

 

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689464488"></a>
#### Allotment of shares for non-cash consideration

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689467816"></a>
##### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.12.1"></a>FAQ 51.12.1 – How should shares issued for non-cash consideration be accounted for?

Reference to standard: IAS 32 para 2  
 Reference to standing text: [51.12](85.md#s350-Para_51.12) 

  

  
Companies might allot shares, including any premium, for money or for other items that can be valued in money, such as fixed assets or intellectual property. In these circumstances, there might be a requirement for the non-cash consideration to be valued, to determine the amount to be recognised as paid-in capital. This amount is split between the share’s par value and share premium, where this is required by national law. In some cases, the local legal valuation requirement might be different from the accounting value for the consideration received. In this case, a balancing credit or debit in equity might be required. The legal significance of this debit or credit will depend on local requirements.

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689212328"></a>
#### Capital redemption reserve

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689217448"></a>
##### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.17.1"></a>FAQ 51.17.1 – Why might a capital redemption reserve need to be created?

 

Reference to standard: IAS 32 para 2  
Reference to standing text: [51.17](85.md#s350-Para_51.17)  

  

  
Reserves might need to be created, in some jurisdictions, for capital maintenance purposes in situations such as after the redemption or purchase of their own shares. Companies in such jurisdictions transfer the nominal (par) value of the shares acquired or cancelled (including cancelling treasury shares) to a capital redemption reserve.

 

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689223976"></a>
#### Capital contributions

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689222952"></a>
##### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.18.1"></a>FAQ 51.18.1 – How is a capital contribution accounted for?

Reference to standard: IAS 32 para 2  
 Reference to standing text: [51.18](85.md#s350-Para_51.18) 

  

  
Contributions from owners, in their capacity as owners of the entity, are distinguished from transfers that arise from trading activities in the normal course of business. Contributions from owners are non-reciprocal in nature. In essence, they are a gift as there is no contractual obligation to repay it. They can be assets or services given or liabilities forgiven, without the transferee being obliged to give anything of benefit in exchange.

Such contributions are an increase in equity. Typically, such amounts are recorded in a separate reserve, often known as the ‘capital reserve’.

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689231144"></a>
#### Distributions/dividends

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689228840"></a>
##### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.22.1"></a>FAQ 51.22.1 – What form can a distribution take?

Reference to standard: IAS 32 para 35  
 Reference to standing text: [51.22](85.md#s350-Para_51.22) 

  

  
Distributions by an entity to its equity instrument holders are usually dividends or returns of capital (such as share buybacks), but they might take other forms. Distributions are made at the entity’s directors’ discretion, often subject to shareholders’ approval.

Distributions, similar to contributions, are non-reciprocal transfers. They can also be made for non-cash consideration. For example, the transfer of a subsidiary’s trade and assets to a parent might be, in substance, a distribution in kind (a dividend in kind – see [para 51.24](85.md#s350-Para_51.24) above).

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689238184"></a>
##### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.22.2"></a>FAQ 51.22.2 – When should a dividend be recognised?

Reference to standard: IAS 32 para 2  
 Reference to standing text: [51.22](85.md#s350-Para_51.22) 

  

  
The decision about when to recognise a dividend as appropriately authorised, and no longer at the entity’s discretion, depends on an entity’s constitution and/or the national law of the territory in which it is established. In some jurisdictions, where a members’ resolution is required to cancel a dividend, the declaration of a dividend by the board of directors creates a liability for the company. The members, and not management, have discretion not to pay the dividend. In other jurisdictions, the members in general meeting approve the dividend that the directors have proposed. There is no obligation to pay until the members’ approval has been obtained.

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689250600"></a>
##### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.22.3"></a>FAQ 51.22.3 – How should scrip dividends be accounted for?

Reference to standard: IAS 32 para 2  
 Reference to standing text: [51.22](85.md#s350-Para_51.22) 

  

  
 Many publicly traded companies make arrangements for ordinary shareholders to elect to receive their dividends in additional ordinary shares rather than in cash – a scrip or stock dividend. A short-term advantage of this is that it improves the company’s cash position. Such dividends are recognised in the financial statements at the amount of the cash alternative. The notes to the financial statements should give a full sufficient explanation of the situation to explain the nature of the scrip dividend and what amounts were paid in shares.

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689264424"></a>
#### Treasury shares

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689263656"></a>
##### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.33.1"></a>FAQ 51.33.1 – How should the liability for acquisition of own shares be measured?

 

Reference to standard: IAS 32 para 23; IFRS 9 B5.4.6, (IAS 39 para AG8)  
 Reference to standing text: [51.33](85.md#s350-Para_51.33) 

  

  
 If the contract specifies the number rather than the value of the shares to be acquired, the financial liability is initially measured using an estimate of the share price that the entity will have to pay. The maximum number of shares to be acquired under the contract is used, as the company cannot avoid delivering cash for that maximum number of shares. The best estimate of the share price is the price at inception of the contract. The financial liability is subsequently adjusted to reflect movements in the share price. There will, therefore, be gains or losses to be recognised in profit or loss, because paragraph B5.4.6 of IFRS 9 (AG8 of IAS 39) requires the carrying value of financial liabilities to be adjusted through the income statement for changes in the measurement of that liability.. However, if the total number of shares bought back is less than the maximum amount, the difference is accounted for in the same way as an option that expires unexercised, by a reclassification from liability to equity.

If the contract caps the amount that can be spent, the financial liability is measured at that maximum amount, discounted for the time value of money for the period between the inception of the contract and the date of payment. In this case, the effect on profit or loss could be limited to unwinding the discount. If the amount spent is less than the maximum amount, the difference is accounted for an option that expires unexercised, by a reclassification from liability to equity.

 

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1689329576"></a>
##### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.37.1"></a>FAQ 51.37.1 – How should shares held in group undertakings be treated in individual financial statements?

Reference to standard: IAS 32 para 2  
 Reference to standing text: [51.37](85.md#s350-Para_51.37) 

  

  
 In the individual financial statements of a subsidiary that holds shares in its parent, such shares are accounted for as financial assets in accordance with IFRS 9 (IAS 39). ‘Treasury shares’ accounting does not apply, because these are not ‘own shares’ for the subsidiary.

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1702210456"></a>
#### Reserves

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1702209560"></a>
##### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.45.1"></a>FAQ 51.45.1 – What is usually included in retained earnings?

 

Reference to standard: IAS 32 para 7  
 Reference to standing text: [51.45](85.md#s351-Para_51.45) 

  

  
Companies use a variety of terms in relation to what IAS 1 calls ‘retained earnings’. The terms ‘profit and loss account’, ‘revenue reserves’, ‘retained earnings’ and ‘retained profits’ are generally accepted and synonymous.

The cumulative effect on earnings, of changes in accounting policy and the correction of errors, is reflected in retained earnings. Other effects on equity, arising from credits for equity share-based payments or actuarial gains and losses on defined benefit pension plans, are usually dealt with in retained earnings.

 

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1702221592"></a>
#### Disclosure requirements

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1702216600"></a>
##### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.48.1"></a>FAQ 51.48.1 – Do the disclosure requirements only apply to share capital accounted for as equity?

Reference to standard: IAS 1 paras 79, 80  
 Reference to standing text: [51.48](85.md#s352-Para_51.48) 

  

  
IAS 1 has specific disclosure requirements for share capital. Paragraph 79 of IAS 1 does not state whether these disclosure requirements apply only to share capital that is accounted for as equity, or whether they also apply to share capital recognised as financial liabilities. In our view, these disclosures relate only to shares classified as equity. This is because the line items recognised on the face of the balance sheet include ‘issued capital and reserves attributable to owners of the parent’ and ‘financial liabilities’. Disclosures for financial liabilities are addressed in IFRS 7.

In addition, paragraph 80 of IAS 1, which applies equivalent disclosures to an entity without share capital, requires such entities to disclose information about ‘each category of equity interest’. However, management might regard shares classified as financial liabilities as part of an entity’s capital. IAS 1 requires additional disclosures on capital. It might be appropriate to provide similar details on non-equity share capital, to give a balanced picture.

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1702239640"></a>
##### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.48.2"></a>FAQ 51.48.2 – What are the requirements if an entity (such as a partnership or trust) does not have share capital?

 

Reference to standard: IAS 1 para 80  
 Reference to standing text: [51.48](85.md#s352-Para_51.48) 

  

  
Where an entity, such as a partnership or trust, does not have share capital, it discloses, for each category of equity interest, information equivalent to that required by paragraph 79(a). [See paragraph 51.48](85.md#s352-Para_51.48).

 

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1702246552"></a>
##### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.53.1"></a>FAQ 51.53.1 – What would be included in the reconciliation of reserves?

Reference to standard: IAS 1 para 106  
 Reference to standing text: [51.53](85.md#s352-Para_51.53) 

  

  
 For each component (such as the balance of retained earnings – that is, accumulated profit or loss), different types of movement are separately broken down. The statement of changes in equity also shows transfers between different components of equity. For example, the statement shows movements between the revaluation reserve and the retained profits reserve in respect of previous revaluation surpluses on property, plant and equipment that have been realised through depreciation or sale in the year.

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1702251544"></a>
##### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.53.2"></a>FAQ 51.53.2 – What is required to be disclosed in respect of distributable reserves?

Reference to standard: IAS 1 para 106  
 Reference to standing text: [51.53](85.md#s352-Para_51.53) 

  

  
Reserves do not need to be disclosed as either realised or unrealised under IFRS. Where there is a material difference between the balance on the retained profits reserve and the amount that is legally distributable, management might consider it appropriate to disclose, in the financial statements, the amounts of distributable and non-distributable reserves respectively.

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1702259352"></a>
##### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.58.1"></a>FAQ 51.58.1 – How is capital defined?

 

Reference to standard: IAS 1 para 134  
 Reference to standing text: [51.58](85.md#s352-Para_51.58) 

  

  
 ‘Capital’ is not defined in IFRS. As noted in [paragraph 51.57](85.md#s352-Para_51.57), an entity is required to describe what it manages as capital on the basis of information provided internally to key management personnel. Whilst capital would often equate with equity for the purposes of this disclosure, it might also include some financial liabilities or exclude some components of equity. For example,paragraph 135(b) of IAS 1 notes that some entities regard certain forms of subordinated debt as part of capital, while others exclude the cash flow hedge reserve. (This might be because amounts deferred therein are not permanently retained in equity. Amounts relating to net investment hedges and fair value reserves are more likely to be regarded as capital, because they are in respect of recognised assets and liabilities and are only reclassified to the income statement on sale.) In practice, some entities also include interest-bearing debt as part of capital.

 

<a id="s354-chap_51_navPoint_idp1702202776"></a>
##### <a id="s354-FAQ_51.58.2"></a>FAQ 51.58.2 – Are the requirements applicable to all entities?

 

Reference to standard: IAS 1 para 134  
 Reference to standing text: [51.58](85.md#s352-Para_51.58) 

  

  
 The Basis for Conclusions section of IAS 1 makes clear that the capital disclosures apply to all entities and not just those subject to external regulation. [IAS 1 para BC87]. This means that the scope of the ‘externally imposed capital requirements’ is broad and includes not only solvency ratios established by insurance or banking regulators, but also capital requirements/limits established by other bodies and through certain contractual relationships. For example, a requirement to accelerate repayment of a loan if certain criteria (‘covenants’) are violated is an externally imposed capital requirement, where the loan itself is included in the entity’s definition of capital. It is not the inclusion of components of capital in the criteria themselves (such as the maintenance of a certain debt/equity ratio) that are the requirements in question, but rather it is the application of those criteria to a loan that is included in the entity’s definition of capital that is at issue. Thus, although a covenant test might be computed by reference to a ‘capital ratio’ (such as debt/equity), this would not in itself represent an externally imposed capital requirement, unless the loan to which the covenant relates is part of the entity’s capital. Disclosure requirements regarding covenants for loans that are not included in the entity’s capital are contained in IFRS 7 and are discussed further in [chapter 47 para 10](78.md#s307-Para_47.10) and chapter 6.9A para 15.
